Executive Summary

This audit was not commissioned. We ran it because a national sweep of low-rated franchise dealerships across 54 metros surfaced Central Houston Cadillac, and the split we found is the starkest of any store in the sample.

Your Google presence is not merely maintained — it is unusually good. 4.4 stars on 3,169 reviews, and the owner replies are genuinely well written. One recent reply opens by comparing a late-running deal to a Rockets game that went into overtime. Another responds to a complaint by publishing the General Sales Manager's direct email so the customer can escalate. Whoever writes those is good at this.

On the Better Business Bureau, the same company has not answered a single published complaint in over a year. We pulled the ten most recent complaints BBB lists. All ten are marked Unanswered, running from July 2025 through June 2026. The stated reason for the F is exactly that: fourteen complaints never answered.

Yelp tells the same story — 2.1 stars on 201 reviews, claimed, with 131 further reviews suppressed by Yelp's filter.

So the finding here is not that the store is badly run. It is that a demonstrably capable communications habit is pointed at exactly one platform, and the two platforms a suspicious shopper checks first are unattended. There is also a concrete data defect: your BBB and trade-association records carry a different street address than your Google listing. Section 4 covers it.

3What the Complaints Actually Say

We read the published texts rather than just the counts, because the pattern here is unusually specific and it is not a service-quality pattern.

Stated plainly before anything else in this section.

Everything below is an allegation made by a member of the public on a platform that publishes complaints without adjudicating them. Circuit Coders takes no position on whether any of it is accurate, and we are not lawyers.

The dominant theme — cancellation refunds on F&I products

Six of the ten most recent complaints are "Product Issues," and the majority describe the same sequence: a customer cancels GAP coverage or a maintenance plan after an early payoff or a trade-in, completes the paperwork, and then waits for a refund that does not arrive.

  • June 2026 — unearned GAP waiver charges after early termination of a conditional sales contract.
  • April 2026 — refund owed after cancelling GAP and a maintenance plan following a trade-in; complainant states the cancellation paperwork was completed.
  • December 2025 — a request for $2,080 in claimed overcharges on an October 2025 purchase.

This matters more than an ordinary complaint cluster because unearned GAP refunds are regulated. A pattern of unreturned cancellation refunds is the kind of thing that attracts attention beyond BBB, and it is fixable with a process rather than an apology. We are not lawyers and this is not legal advice — but of everything in this document, it is the item we would look at first regardless of whether you ever hire us.

Secondary theme — service duration

A May 2026 complainant describes a vehicle held over two months for a transmission repair with communication breakdowns throughout. An October 2025 complainant describes a scheduled 9:30am service with no update until early afternoon.

One that is worth reading personally

An October 2025 complaint documents what the customer describes as the mistreatment of a military buyer during a straightforward cash transaction, naming a manager. It is unanswered. Whatever happened, a public, unrebutted allegation of that specific kind sitting on a page that ranks for your store's name is worth thirty minutes of somebody's time.

Name, address and phone consistency is the input Google weighs most heavily when deciding whether two listings describe the same real business. A trade-association record pointing at a different street — roughly six miles away — is the kind of stale data that propagates into aggregators and quietly splits your local signal.

Related: the same directory lists the dealership's published contact email on a personal AOL address rather than a dealership domain. That is not a reputation problem in itself, but it is very often the same root cause as the BBB routing failure in Section 2 — official notices going to an inbox that is nobody's job.

Also worth one sentence: Central Houston Nissan appears under the same ownership in that directory, at 2901 South Loop West, carrying 4.2 stars on 8,806 Google reviews. We have not audited it and make no claims about it. But if the BBB routing is broken at one rooftop, it is worth checking whether it is broken at both.

5What Is Genuinely Well Run

Credit where it is earned — and it is earned unusually clearly here.
  • The Google review responses are the best we have read in this campaign. They are warm, specific, non-templated, and they name individual staff. Whoever writes them should be the person we hand the Yelp and BBB voice to — we would be copying an in-house standard, not importing one.
  • 3,169 reviews at 4.4 is a strong base for a luxury single-point store.
  • Staff are named positively and repeatedly — Mike Lahlou in sales, Javier in service, Kevin in follow-up. A reviewer wrote that Kevin "follows up after service and makes sure you're completely satisfied. That's rare these days."
  • The store publishes a real escalation path when a customer is unhappy on Google. Most dealerships never do this.

7The Program

Everything below is work we do, not advice we hand you.

Recovery sprint — the first 30 days

  • Diagnose the notice routing first. Fourteen unanswered complaints at a store that writes excellent Google replies is almost certainly a mailbox problem. We find out where BBB and Yelp notifications are landing before we draft a word.
  • Answer the BBB backlog. All 14 unanswered complaints drafted, reviewed by you, filed.
  • Triage the refund cluster separately and first. The GAP and maintenance-plan cancellation complaints are not a messaging problem. We list them, match them to your F&I records, and hand you the reconciliation — before any public response goes out.
  • Open Yelp. Response templates for the existing 201, then every new review answered inside 48 hours, in the voice your Google replies already use.
  • Correct the address record with HADA and any aggregator carrying 2520 Main Street, and move the published contact email to a dealership domain.
  • NAP reconciliation across Google, Yelp, BBB, KBB, Cars.com, DealerRater, CARFAX, Apple and Bing.

Ongoing, monthly

  • Every review on every platform answered within 48 hours, in your voice, signed by a real person.
  • A review pipeline pointed at the unmanaged platforms — the fix for Yelp is not arguing with the reviews you have, it is giving the next several hundred satisfied buyers somewhere to go.
  • BBB complaint monitoring with a named owner, so the grade never regenerates from silence.
  • Listing integrity monitoring — new duplicates, drifting hours, changed numbers.
  • Monthly written report: what moved, what did not, what we recommend next. Plain English.

10Method

So you can check every number rather than trust it.

  • Discovery. BBB's public search API, filtered to F-rated new- and used-car dealers across 54 U.S. metros. 3,368 low-rated dealerships returned; 1,128 franchise rooftops after removing national chains and manufacturer corporate records.
  • BBB. Public profile and complaints pages, read in full. Rating reasons, complaint status counts, business details and contacts quoted verbatim.
  • Google. Business Profile and Maps listing read directly, including the review summary panel, owner-response behaviour and the local competitive set.
  • Yelp. Live listing, first page of reviews read in full, owner-response state and suppressed-review count taken from the page itself.
  • Trade records. Houston Automobile Dealers Association published member directory, for the address and contact discrepancy in Section 4.
  • No access, no scraping of private systems, no contact with your staff or customers. Everything here was readable by anyone with a browser.
