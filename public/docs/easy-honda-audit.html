Executive Summary

This audit was not commissioned. We ran it because a national sweep of low-rated franchise dealerships across 54 metros returned Easy Honda. Then we read the detail, and the honest conclusion is narrower — and better for you — than the F implies.

You are not the store we usually find on this list. Most dealerships in this sweep ignore BBB outright; several have gone twelve to eighteen consecutive months without answering a single published complaint. You have not. Of your ten most recent complaints, seven are marked Answered or Resolved. BBB's stated reason for the F is three unanswered complaints out of thirteen filed. That is a small backlog, and we are not going to inflate it.

Your Google presence is genuinely strong — 4.5 stars on 5,578 reviews, one of the larger single-rooftop bases in this entire sweep.

The finding is one unattended platform. Yelp rates the same store 2.5 on 170 reviews, with 40 more suppressed by their filter — a two-star gap describing the same building and the same people. The page is already claimed, so nothing is blocking you.

One complaint theme sits outside review management and we would rather flag it than sell around it: cancellation refunds on F&I products. Section 3 covers it plainly.

2Yelp — Two Stars Below Everything Else You Do

Highest-leverage defect
170 Yelp reviewers at 2.5 stars are setting the first impression of a store that 5,578 Google reviewers rate 4.5.

Yelp carries roughly 3% of your total review volume and sits two full stars below the platform carrying the rest.

Three things make this the cheapest fix in the document:

  • The page is already claimed. Somebody at the store has the login. No recovery process, no waiting on Yelp support.
  • You already know how to do this work. Seven of ten BBB complaints engaged is the proof. The capability exists — it has simply never been pointed at Yelp.
  • 40 reviews are sitting suppressed. Yelp's filter has moved them into "not currently recommended," where they do not count toward the score. That filter disproportionately buries reviews from low-activity accounts — exactly what somebody who buys one car every six years looks like.

5,578 Google reviews did not happen by accident. Something in your process asks for them. Pointing that same process at the platform sitting two stars lower is the whole recommendation.

The theme worth your attention — F&I cancellation refunds

This is the one we would look at first, and it is the one review management cannot fix:

  • A November 2024 complainant states they completed warranty cancellation paperwork in September 2023 for a car they no longer owned, and describes chasing the refund afterward. This one is unanswered.
  • An April 2025 complainant describes two service contracts purchased alongside a 2025 Pilot Black Edition — a $695 protection plan and a windshield product — and a dispute over them. That one you carried to Resolved.

The contrast in those two outcomes is the entire argument of this document: where you engaged, it closed properly; where you did not, it is still sitting public. Unearned cancellation refunds are also regulated, which is why we would resolve the open one regardless of whether you ever hire us.

Theme two — what was represented at the sale

  • A February 2025 complainant alleges a mileage discrepancy on a car bought in January 2023, discovered later.
  • A January 2024 complainant states the vehicle was not as advertised and that the engine failed on the drive home.
  • An April 2026 complainant describes paying roughly $4,000 down, taking possession, and a subsequent dispute over the financing approval. Unanswered.

We are not adjudicating any of these. But the April 2026 one is both recent and documentary — it turns on your own financing records — and it is the newest unanswered item on the page.

6The Program

Everything below is work we do, not advice we hand you.

Recovery sprint — the first 30 days

  • Open Yelp first. The page is claimed, so this starts day one: response templates for the existing 170, then every new review answered inside 48 hours.
  • Clear the three unanswered BBB complaints. An afternoon of work that moves the letter grade, given the rest of your file is already engaged.
  • Resolve the open refund complaint specifically. The November 2024 warranty-cancellation item goes to you directly in week one with the internal question written out.
  • Answer the April 2026 financing complaint. Newest unanswered item, turns on your own records, and it is the one a shopper doing homework reads first.
  • Attack the suppressed pile. 40 filtered reviews re-weight as legitimate accounts gain activity; the fix is volume from real buyers, not argument.
  • NAP reconciliation across Google, Yelp, BBB, KBB, Cars.com, DealerRater, CARFAX, Apple and Bing.

Ongoing, monthly

  • Every review on every platform answered within 48 hours, in your voice, signed by a real person.
  • A review pipeline pointed at the unmanaged platforms — the fix for Yelp is not arguing with the reviews you have, it is giving the next several hundred satisfied buyers somewhere to go.
  • BBB complaint monitoring with a named owner, so the grade never regenerates from silence.
  • Listing integrity monitoring — new duplicates, drifting hours, changed numbers.
  • Monthly written report: what moved, what did not, what we recommend next. Plain English.

9Method

So you can check every number rather than trust it.

  • Discovery. BBB's public search API, filtered to F-rated new- and used-car dealers across 54 U.S. metros. 3,368 low-rated dealerships returned; 1,128 franchise rooftops after removing national chains and manufacturer corporate records.
  • BBB. Public profile and complaints pages, read in full. Rating reasons, complaint status counts, business details and contacts quoted verbatim.
  • Google. Business Profile and Maps listing read directly, including the review summary panel, owner-response behaviour and the local competitive set.
  • Yelp. Live listing, first page of reviews read in full, owner-response state and suppressed-review count taken from the page itself.
  • No access, no scraping of private systems, no contact with your staff or customers. Everything here was readable by anyone with a browser.
