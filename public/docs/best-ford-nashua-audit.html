1What We Found

1.1 · Your website and your listings publish different phone numbers

Your site advertises three toll-free numbers — 888-432-2696 (service), 888-810-6020 (sales), 888-749-3847 (parts). Every directory we checked lists your local line, (603) 889-0161.

Call tracking is normal and useful. The issue is that Google treats the number on your website as a primary identity signal and cross-checks it against your listings. When they disagree, confidence in the record falls — and a toll-free number carries no local signal at all, which for a store competing on "Ford dealer near me" is the one thing you cannot afford to give away.

Highest-leverage fix on this page

It costs nothing, it does not mean giving up call tracking, and it is the correction most likely to move local ranking on its own. The local number becomes primary; the tracked numbers stay exactly where they earn their keep.

1.2 · Your homepage never says "Nashua"

Your page title does its job — "New & Used Ford Dealership In Nashua, NH | Best Ford Nashua". But the headline a visitor and a crawler both read first is just "Welcome To Best Ford".

The H1 is the strongest single on-page statement of what a page is about. Yours says what you are but not where. "Welcome to Best Ford in Nashua, NH" is a one-line change on a Dealer Inspire site.

1.3 · "Best Ford Lincoln Mercury" is still attached to you

Your own site footer still references Best Ford Lincoln Mercury. Mercury was discontinued in 2011. That name almost certainly still exists as live listings and stale citations across directories, review sites and aggregators — every one an address record competing with your real one, and holding reviews that should be consolidating onto your current listing.

We have fixed this exact pattern before: a legacy dual-brand name left behind after a rebrand, quietly splitting a store's reputation across two identities.

3Why We Think There Is Money Here

Across every dealership we have audited, one pattern holds without exception: Yelp and the secondary platforms run well below Google and DealerRater, because nobody owns them. You have 140 Yelp reviews and, on the pattern, very likely no response history. Every unanswered complaint is a permanent, indexed page about your store that a shopper reads before they call you.

You have also been under new ownership since 2022. New ownership inherits listing debt from the previous regime — old hours, old names, old numbers, unclaimed profiles. It never surfaces until someone goes looking, and nobody has.
