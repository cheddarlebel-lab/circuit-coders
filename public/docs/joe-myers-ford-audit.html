Executive Summary

This audit was not commissioned. We ran it because a national sweep of low-rated franchise dealerships across 54 metros surfaced Joe Myers Ford, and what we found was not what the F rating implied.

Your store is not badly rated. It is unevenly rated. On Google, Joe Myers Ford sits at 4.4 stars on 8,464 reviews — a very large base for a single rooftop — and someone on your team replies to those reviews, including the critical ones, at length and by name. That is genuinely good work and it is the reason this document is worth reading: it proves the store already knows how to do this.

On the platforms where nobody is working, the picture inverts completely. Yelp is 2.1 stars on 407 reviews. The Better Business Bureau grades you F for one stated reason: 21 complaints were never answered. We pulled the ten most recent complaints BBB publishes for your store. All ten are marked Unanswered. Not some — all ten, running from June 2025 through March 2026.

That is the finding that matters most here, and it is worth separating from everything else: this is not a business that responds badly. On BBB, over the last nine months, it does not respond at all. Meanwhile the same company is writing thoughtful, personalised replies on Google the same week. One channel has an owner and the other does not.

You have been in business fifty years. The F is attached to that name on a page that ranks for it.

2The BBB F — Ten of Ten Unanswered

Hard defect
Every one of the ten most recent complaints BBB publishes for Joe Myers Ford is marked "Unanswered."

BBB's stated reasons, verbatim: "Failure to respond to 21 complaint(s) filed against business" and "26 complaint(s) filed against business." The business is not BBB accredited and the profile carries an alert.

We checked each of the ten individually rather than trusting the summary. The dates run from 06/07/2025 through 03/13/2026 — nine consecutive months in which no published complaint received a business response.

BBB's algorithm punishes silence far more heavily than it punishes the underlying complaint. A business that answers all twenty-six and resolves none would score better than where you sit now. This is the cheapest item on the lot to fix and the most expensive to leave alone.

The likeliest explanation is routing, not indifference.

A store that replies to Google reviews within days is not choosing to ignore BBB. Far more often the BBB notices are arriving at an address nobody monitors — a departed employee, a general mailbox, a spam folder. That is a fifteen-minute diagnosis and it is the first thing we would check.

3What the Complaints Actually Say

We read the published texts rather than just the counts.

Stated plainly before anything else in this section.

Everything below is an allegation made by a member of the public on a platform that publishes complaints without adjudicating them. Circuit Coders takes no position on whether any of it is accurate, and we are not lawyers. We flag it because these words currently sit on a page that ranks for your store's name.

Theme one — add-ons billed but allegedly not performed

A January 2026 buyer states they were charged for window tint, paint sealant, paint protection film and a spray-in bedliner that the dealership recorded as completed. This is the most actionable complaint in the file: it is specific, it is documentary, and it is either true or trivially disproved by a work order.

Theme two — service duration and communication

Five of the ten are service or repair issues. The recurring shape is a vehicle held for an extended period with the customer chasing updates. One reviewer on Google describes a truck in the shop for two months — and notably, that one got a public reply. The BBB versions did not.

Theme three — the phones

A June 2025 complainant writes that nobody in the finance department will return calls about a referral check. An independent Google reviewer describes an inventory manager who "ruined the sale by showing no effort." Neither is a product problem. Both are follow-up problems, and both are the kind of thing that never reaches a GM because the people who hit it simply leave.

Theme four — a protection plan's scope

A March 2026 complainant states they bought a mechanical protection plan believing it covered routine service, then found it did not. Whether or not the plan was described accurately, the complaint is a disclosure-timing signal — the same category we would want to review in the F&I presentation.

4What Is Genuinely Well Run

Credit where it is earned.

A store with real problems on the floor does not hold 4.4 stars across 8,464 reviews for fifty years. What follows is a list of things we would not change.

  • Your Google review response is the model. Replies are specific, signed, and — crucially — the negative ones get real answers rather than a template. That is the hardest habit to build in a dealership and you already have it. Our entire BBB and Yelp proposal is "do what you already do on Google, over there."
  • 8,464 reviews is a major asset. Very few single rooftops carry that base.
  • Fifty years in business. Longevity is the one thing no competitor can buy, and the Joe Myers name still anchors the Houston market.
  • Named staff are praised repeatedly — William Garret and James Avesta both appear by name in recent five-star reviews describing smooth, low-pressure purchases.

6The Program

Everything below is work we do, not advice we hand you.

Recovery sprint — the first 30 days

  • Diagnose the BBB routing first. Before drafting anything we find out where BBB notices are actually landing. Twenty-one unanswered complaints at a store that answers Google is a plumbing problem.
  • Answer the backlog. All 21 unanswered complaints drafted, reviewed by you, filed. Where a complaint is resolvable, we pursue the resolution, not just the reply.
  • Open Yelp. Response templates for the existing 407, then every new review answered inside 48 hours.
  • Pull the work orders on the add-on complaint. The January 2026 tint/sealant/PPF/bedliner allegation is either disproved by a document or it is real. Both outcomes are worth knowing this week.
  • NAP reconciliation across Google, Yelp, BBB, KBB, Cars.com, DealerRater, CARFAX, Apple and Bing.
  • Disclosure review on the protection-plan language in Section 3.

Ongoing, monthly

  • Every review on every platform answered within 48 hours, in your voice, signed by a real person.
  • A review pipeline pointed at the unmanaged platforms — the fix for Yelp is not arguing with the reviews you have, it is giving the next several hundred satisfied buyers somewhere to go.
  • BBB complaint monitoring with a named owner, so the grade never regenerates from silence.
  • Listing integrity monitoring — new duplicates, drifting hours, changed numbers.
  • Monthly written report: what moved, what did not, what we recommend next. Plain English.

9Method

So you can check every number rather than trust it.

  • Discovery. BBB's public search API, filtered to F-rated new- and used-car dealers across 54 U.S. metros. 3,368 low-rated dealerships returned; 1,128 franchise rooftops after removing national chains and manufacturer corporate records.
  • BBB. Public profile and complaints pages, read in full. Rating reasons, complaint status counts, business details and contacts quoted verbatim.
  • Google. Business Profile and Maps listing read directly, including the review summary panel, owner-response behaviour and the local competitive set.
  • Yelp. Live listing, first page of reviews read in full, owner-response state and suppressed-review count taken from the page itself.
  • No access, no scraping of private systems, no contact with your staff or customers. Everything here was readable by anyone with a browser.
