Executive Summary

This audit was not commissioned. We ran it because a national sweep of low-rated franchise dealerships surfaced Keating Toyota at the very bottom, and the reason turned out to be more interesting than the ranking.

Keating Toyota holds the lowest BBB rating score of all 3,368 low-rated dealerships we measured across 54 U.S. metros. The score is 4.0 out of 100. We expected to find a badly run store. That is not what the evidence shows.

Your store is not badly rated. It is unevenly rated, and the split is the widest we have measured anywhere. On Google — the platform your CRM solicits after a sale — Keating Toyota sits at 4.5 stars on 9,109 reviews. That is an enormous, healthy review base that very few single rooftops in the country carry. On Yelp, which nobody is working, the same store sits at 1.9 stars on 397 reviews. That is a 2.6-star gap between two platforms describing the same building.

The cost of the split is that the unmanaged surfaces are the ones a stranger hits first. A shopper checking whether you are legitimate finds the 1.9 and the F. The 4.5 sits on a platform they reach only after they have already decided to trust you.

One finding sits outside the reputation frame and we would rather say it than sell around it. The complaints driving the BBB grade are not scattered — they cluster hard on add-on products presented as mandatory and the gap between an advertised price and the financed number. The same two allegations appear independently on Yelp, from different people, in different years, and several complainants state they have filed with the Texas Attorney General and the FTC. Review management does not fix that. Section 4 covers it plainly, because a vendor who sold you review-generation and stayed quiet about it would be taking your money under false pretenses.

The platform at 1.9 is the one that only receives reviews people take the trouble to leave unprompted — which, without a pipeline pointed at it, means almost exclusively the aggrieved. A 2.6-star spread is not a rounding difference. It is two different stores as far as a stranger can tell, and the shopper meets the 1.9 first.

2Yelp — 1.9 Stars, Claimed, and 127 Reviews Buried

Highest-leverage defect
397 Yelp reviewers at 1.9 stars are setting the first impression of a store that 9,109 Google reviewers rate 4.5.

Yelp carries roughly 4% of your total review volume and sits 2.6 stars below the platform carrying the rest.

Three things make this the cheapest fix in the document:

  • The page is already claimed. Somebody at the store has the login. No recovery process, no waiting on Yelp support.
  • The reviews are unanswered. We read the full first page — July 2026 back to August 2025. On Google you engage; here the capability has simply never been pointed at the platform.
  • 127 reviews are sitting suppressed. Yelp's filter has moved 127 reviews — about a quarter of your total base there — into "not currently recommended," where they do not count toward the score. That filter disproportionately buries reviews from low-activity accounts, which is exactly what somebody who buys one car every six years looks like.

Worth noting in your favour: the recent Yelp reviews are not uniformly hostile. A July 8, 2026 reviewer describes an outstanding purchase and singles out Jessica in the title department for taking ownership of a difficult out-of-state registration — "she stood by her word." The platform is recoverable. It has just never been worked.

3The BBB F — One Sentence Explains It

Hard defect
Keating Toyota holds an F from the Better Business Bureau with a rating score of 4.0 out of 100 — the lowest in a 3,368-dealer national sample.

BBB's two stated reasons, verbatim: "Failure to respond to 12 complaint(s) filed against business" and "6 complaint(s) filed against business that were not resolved." The business is not BBB accredited, and the profile carries a Pattern of Complaints alert.

This is the single most fixable item here, and the most expensive to leave alone. BBB's algorithm punishes silence far more heavily than it punishes the underlying complaint — a business that answers every complaint and resolves none still outranks one that ignores twelve.

Twelve unanswered disputes is not a quality failure. It is an inbox nobody owns. BBB routes complaint notices to the contact of record; if that routing has drifted or lands somewhere nobody checks, the F will keep regenerating no matter how well the store performs on the floor.

The Pattern of Complaints alert is the part that compounds.

BBB applies that flag when it judges that a business has failed to resolve the underlying cause of repeated complaints, rather than any single incident. It is the difference between a grade you can answer your way out of and one that requires a process change. Section 4 is our honest read of what that cause appears to be.

4What the Complaints Actually Say

We read the published complaint texts rather than just the counts, because the counts alone would have led us to sell you the wrong thing.

Stated plainly before anything else in this section.

Everything below is an allegation made by a member of the public on platforms that publish complaints without adjudicating them. Circuit Coders takes no position on whether any of it is accurate, and we are not lawyers. We are flagging it because these are the words currently sitting on pages that rank for your store's name — and because the same theme repeats across independent complainants, on two different platforms, in different years.

Theme one — add-on products described as mandatory

This is the dominant theme, and it appears on BBB and Yelp independently:

  • A BBB complainant describes a non-refundable GPS product at $5,829 and a windshield-protection charge added in F&I, discovered after the fact.
  • A second BBB complainant, a year earlier, writes that the same GPS add-on "they say is mandatory when it is not."
  • A Yelp reviewer describes being told a window-tint package would be "thrown in for free," then finding a $2,500 charge for it on the paperwork.
  • Another describes roughly $11,000 in additional products on a Tundra lease, including a ~$6,300 anti-theft item.
  • A further reviewer alleges a service contract was presented as a condition of sale, and cites Texas Occupations Code § 1304.006 by name.

Theme two — advertised price versus financed number

  • A truck advertised at $27,795, presented with roughly $7,000 in add-ons at the desk.
  • A Yelp reviewer: listed at $23,394, contract presented at $26,100.
  • Another: agreed at roughly $37,000 out the door, finance total arriving near $60,000.

Again — we are not adjudicating these. But the consistent shape is a disclosure-timing problem: the qualifying condition exists somewhere, and buyers are meeting it in the finance office instead of in the ad. Several complainants state they have filed with the Texas Attorney General, the FTC and the Texas DMV Enforcement Division. That is the part that makes this a process question rather than a reputation question.

Said plainly
Answering complaints will move the letter grade. It will not move the Pattern of Complaints alert.

BBB lifts that flag when the underlying cause stops generating complaints. If the F&I presentation stays as it is, the grade will recover and then decay again. Moving one disclosure line from the footer into the offer is close to free, and it retires an entire complaint category. We will mark up the language; adopting it is your call and your call alone.

5What Is Genuinely Well Run

Credit where it is earned — and the reason this is worth doing.

A store with real problems on the floor does not hold 4.5 stars across 9,109 reviews. The operation is working. What follows is a list of things we would not change.

  • Review volume is a genuine asset. 9,109 Google reviews is a top-decile base for a single rooftop anywhere in the country, and it is the raw material a recovery runs on.
  • Your Google rating leads a competitive Houston-market set. Against neighbours: Keating Toyota 4.5 (9,109) · Mac Haik Toyota 4.6 (5,627) · AutoNation Toyota Gulf Freeway 4.5 (9,205) · Mike Calvert Toyota 4.6 (23,018). You are at the front of that group on rating and volume for your trade area.
  • Named staff are repeatedly praised by name — Jessica in titles, and multiple sales and service people across both platforms. Reviewers naming individuals positively is the strongest signal there is that the floor is not the problem.
  • Even critical reviewers describe the facility well. One long, detailed three-star review calls it "by far a nicer dealership than most we visited." The physical product is not in question.

7The Program

Everything below is work we do, not advice we hand you.

Recovery sprint — the first 30 days

  • Answer the BBB backlog. All 12 unanswered complaints drafted, reviewed by you, filed. Where a complaint is resolvable, we pursue the resolution, not just the reply. This alone moves the letter grade.
  • Open Yelp. Response templates for the existing 397, then every new review answered inside 48 hours. Public, non-defensive, signed.
  • Attack the suppressed pile. 127 filtered reviews are not lost — the filter re-weights as legitimate accounts gain activity. We work the pipeline that changes that mix.
  • Correct the BBB record — contact routing, addresses, and accreditation path priced out so you can decide on it with real numbers.
  • NAP reconciliation across Google, Yelp, BBB, KBB, Cars.com, DealerRater, CARFAX, Apple and Bing — one canonical number and address.
  • Disclosure review on the two complaint themes in Section 4. We mark up the current ad and F&I language and hand you a revised version. You decide whether to adopt it.

Ongoing, monthly

  • Every review on every platform answered within 48 hours, in your voice, signed by a real person.
  • A review pipeline pointed at the unmanaged platforms — the fix for Yelp is not arguing with the reviews you have, it is giving the next several hundred satisfied buyers somewhere to go.
  • BBB complaint monitoring with a named owner, so the grade never regenerates from silence.
  • Listing integrity monitoring — new duplicates, drifting hours, changed numbers.
  • Monthly written report: what moved, what did not, what we recommend next. Plain English.

11Method

So you can check every number rather than trust it.

  • Discovery. BBB's public search API, filtered to F-rated new- and used-car dealers across 54 U.S. metros. 3,368 low-rated dealerships returned; 1,128 franchise rooftops after removing national chains and manufacturer corporate records.
  • BBB. Public profile and complaints pages, read in full. Rating reasons, complaint status counts, business details and contacts quoted verbatim.
  • Google. Business Profile and Maps listing read directly, including the review summary panel, owner-response behaviour and the local competitive set.
  • Yelp. Live listing, first page of reviews read in full, owner-response state and suppressed-review count taken from the page itself.
  • Group context. Automotive News Top 150 dealership-group rankings and public trade-press coverage, for ownership and scale only.
  • No access, no scraping of private systems, no contact with your staff or customers. Everything here was readable by anyone with a browser.
