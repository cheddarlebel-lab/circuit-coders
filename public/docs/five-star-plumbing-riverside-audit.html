2The Finding

Critical
He is absent from the only place emergency plumbing demand actually starts.

A Google Maps search for plumbers at 3025 David St, Riverside — his own address — returns thirteen plumbing companies. He is not one of them. When a Riverside homeowner has water on the floor at 9pm, the shortlist they see does not contain him at any position.

This is different from the usual reputation problem, and it is a much easier fix. Nothing has to be repaired. A Google Business Profile has to be created, verified and populated — and seventeen years of goodwill that currently lives entirely in word-of-mouth gets somewhere to accumulate.

9Ad Management — Why It Is Phase 3, Not Phase 1

Ads are the right lever for this business and the wrong thing to sell him first. Both halves matter.

Sequencing
Google Local Services Ads require a verified Google Business Profile.

He does not have one. Selling ad management before the profile exists would mean charging him for work that cannot run. Phase 1 is the prerequisite, not an upsell wrapped around it.

Local Services Ads are the product here — not regular search ads

For a residential plumber, Google Local Services Ads are the strongest placement available. They sit above the map pack and above paid search, they are billed per lead rather than per click, and they carry the Google Guaranteed badge. On an emergency call at 9pm — which is most of this trade — that badge and that position are the whole ballgame.

Google Guaranteed requires verification of licence and insurance. That makes the CSLB lookup in §6 a gating item rather than a nice-to-have: if his licence is not clean and current, Phase 3 cannot happen and we need to know that before it is quoted, not after.

Regular search ads as the secondary layer

A tight search campaign on emergency intent — burst pipe, water heater, sewer backup, no hot water — run against a real landing page from Phase 1. Not brand terms; he has no brand demand to defend yet. This layer only makes sense once there is a site to send clicks to.

The cautionary example, from our own mining this week

Regan Roofing in Temecula is currently buying Google Ads while rated 4.0, against a field sitting at 4.8–5.0. They are paying for clicks that land on a rating which loses the comparison. That is precisely the mistake ad management prevents — and precisely what happens if ads are switched on before reviews and listings are handled.

How the money works

  • $500/mo management fee — campaign build, bid and budget management, lead-quality review, call tracking, monthly reporting. Flat, so our incentive is not to inflate his spend.
  • Ad spend is his, paid directly to Google on his own card. It never passes through Circuit Coders. He keeps ownership of the account and can see every dollar.
  • Suggested opening spend $1,000–1,500/mo on LSA. At a $450 average ticket that needs roughly three to four won jobs to break even before the management fee — a realistic bar for this trade in a market this size, though it is a target and not a promise.
  • No spend commitment. Month-to-month, pausable, and he owns the account if he leaves.

Do not quote a cost-per-lead. LSA pricing varies by trade and market and we have not measured it for Riverside plumbing — quoting a number we have not verified is how the first Crown Toyota draft went wrong.
