Executive Summary

This audit was not commissioned. We ran it because a national sweep of low-rated franchise dealerships across 54 metros returned Northwest Hyundai, and the file turned out to be unusually clean-cut.

Every published complaint on your BBB profile is unanswered. All five of five. BBB's stated reasons are simply "Failure to respond to 5 complaint(s)" and "5 complaint(s) filed" — the numbers are identical because the response rate is zero. They run from July 2025 through June 2026.

That is not what the store looks like anywhere else. On Google, Northwest Hyundai holds 4.3 stars on 5,266 reviews — a large, healthy base that took years to build. On Yelp the same store sits at 2.3 on 151 reviews with 43 more suppressed, a two-star gap describing the same building.

There is a detail on the BBB record that we think explains most of this, and it costs nothing to fix: the business contact is listed only as "Complaint Handler." Not a name. BBB routes complaint notices to whoever is on file — and when that is nobody in particular, the notices land somewhere unowned and the grade regenerates no matter how the store actually performs.

One complaint theme sits outside review management and we would rather flag it than sell around it: charges and protection-plan products disputed after the sale. Section 3 covers it plainly.

The two BBB numbers being identical is the whole story.

"Failure to respond to 5 complaints" and "5 complaints filed" are the same number because not one has ever been answered. There is no partial credit in that file — no Answered, no Resolved, nothing in progress. A single reply would change the shape of the page.

A two-star spread between Google and Yelp is not customers disagreeing about your store. It is one platform your process reaches and two it has never touched.

BBB's algorithm punishes silence far more heavily than the complaint itself. A business that answered all five and resolved none would score materially better than a 0% response rate. This is the cheapest item in the document and the most expensive to leave alone.

"Complaint Handler" is not a person, and that is probably the root cause.

A store with 5,266 Google reviews at 4.3 is not run by people who decide to ignore customers. Far more often the BBB notices arrive at a generic address nobody monitors. Putting a real name on that record is a five-minute change, and until it happens the grade will keep regenerating.

3What the Complaints Actually Say

We read the published texts rather than just the counts.

Stated plainly before anything else in this section.

Everything below is an allegation made by a member of the public on a platform that publishes complaints without adjudicating them. Circuit Coders takes no position on whether any of it is accurate, and we are not lawyers.

Theme one — paperwork that did not get filed

Two of the five are administrative, and both are documentary:

  • An October 2025 buyer states that two months after purchasing a new vehicle, the trade-in paperwork had still not been filed.
  • Another October 2025 complainant describes a lease on a 2025 Tucson SEL and a verbal promise made at signing that they say was not honored.

Neither of these is a matter of opinion. Both are answerable from your own deal jackets today, and unfiled trade-in paperwork is the kind of thing that escalates to a state regulator rather than staying on BBB.

Theme two — F&I products and charges disputed after the sale

  • A June 2026 buyer of a 2026 IONIQ 5 describes purchasing Hyundai Protection Plan products and a subsequent billing dispute.
  • A July 2025 buyer of a 2024 Hyundai states they were charged $1,500 for an Apex product among other issues.

We are not adjudicating either. But the shape — a charge that surfaces as a dispute after delivery — is the one category review management genuinely cannot fix. It is a disclosure-timing question, and moving one line from the paperwork into the conversation earlier retires the whole category.

Theme three — early mechanical failure

An April 2026 buyer states the starter failed within a week of purchase and describes trying to reach the dealership about it. That is a warranty situation, not a sales problem — but it went unanswered, which converts a manufacturer issue into a Northwest Hyundai issue on a page that ranks for your name.

4What Is Genuinely Well Run

Credit where it is earned.

A store with real problems on the floor does not hold 4.3 stars across 5,266 reviews.

  • 5,266 Google reviews at 4.3 is a large base for a single Hyundai rooftop, and it is the raw material any recovery runs on.
  • Something in your process asks for reviews and it works. Volume like that is never accidental. The entire proposal here is to aim that same machine at the two platforms it has never been aimed at.
  • Your Google rating holds up against the local Hyundai set — you sit alongside Humble Hyundai (4.3 on 2,055) and North Freeway Hyundai (4.5 on 3,609) with the largest review base of the three.
  • Only five complaints in the file. For a store doing this volume, five is a low number. The problem is the response rate, not the complaint rate.

6The Program

Everything below is work we do, not advice we hand you.

Recovery sprint — the first 30 days

  • Put a named person on the BBB record. First action, five minutes. Replace "Complaint Handler" and confirm where notices actually land — this is very likely the root cause of a 0% response rate.
  • Answer all five complaints. Drafted, reviewed by you, filed. Five is a small enough backlog to clear in one sitting, and going from 0% to 100% response rate is the single largest move available to your grade.
  • Pull the deal jackets on the two paperwork complaints first. The unfiled trade-in and the lease promise are documentary — answerable today from your own records.
  • Open Yelp. Response templates for the existing 151, then every new review answered inside 48 hours.
  • Attack the suppressed pile. 43 filtered reviews re-weight as legitimate accounts gain activity; the fix is volume from real buyers, not argument.
  • NAP reconciliation across Google, Yelp, BBB, KBB, Cars.com, DealerRater, CARFAX, Apple and Bing.

Ongoing, monthly

  • Every review on every platform answered within 48 hours, in your voice, signed by a real person.
  • A review pipeline pointed at the unmanaged platforms — the fix for Yelp is not arguing with the reviews you have, it is giving the next several hundred satisfied buyers somewhere to go.
  • BBB complaint monitoring with a named owner, so the grade never regenerates from silence.
  • Listing integrity monitoring — new duplicates, drifting hours, changed numbers.
  • Monthly written report: what moved, what did not, what we recommend next. Plain English.
