Executive Summary

This audit was not commissioned. We ran it because a national sweep of low-rated franchise dealerships across 54 metros surfaced Bayway Cadillac Southwest, and what we found is a cleaner version of a pattern we have now seen at more than a dozen stores.

Your store is not badly rated. It is unevenly rated. On Google, Bayway Cadillac Southwest holds 4.4 stars on 4,137 reviews — a very large base for a single luxury rooftop, and the separate service-centre listing does even better at 4.6 on 842. Somebody built that, and it took years.

On the two platforms nobody is working, it inverts hard. The Better Business Bureau grades you F for one stated reason: twelve complaints were never answered. We pulled the ten most recent complaints BBB publishes — all ten are marked Unanswered, running from December 2024 through June 2026. Eighteen consecutive months without a single published response, and the profile carries an alert.

Yelp is 2.8 stars below Google — and you do not control the page. It sits at 1.6 stars on 42 reviews and is unclaimed. That is the lowest rating on any platform in this entire sweep, sitting on a listing nobody at the dealership has ever logged into.

The good news in that: an unclaimed page is the easiest thing in this document to fix. Claiming it costs nothing and takes about ten minutes.

Most dealerships we audit have at least claimed their Yelp listing and simply never worked it. 2The BBB F — Ten of Ten Unanswered

Hard defect
Every one of the ten most recent complaints BBB publishes for this store is marked "Unanswered."

BBB's stated reasons, verbatim: "Failure to respond to 12 complaint(s) filed against business" and "16 complaint(s) filed against business." The profile carries an alert, and BBB lists the business contact only as "Complaint Handler" — not a named person.

BBB's algorithm punishes silence far more heavily than the complaint itself. A business that answers all sixteen and resolves none would score better than the current position.

"Complaint Handler" is not a person.

BBB routes complaint notices to the contact on file. Yours does not name anyone — which is very often the whole explanation for a run like this. Notices arrive somewhere nobody owns, and the grade regenerates no matter how well the store performs on the floor. Putting a real name on that record is a five-minute change.

3What the Complaints Actually Say

We read the published texts rather than just the counts.

Stated plainly before anything else in this section.

Everything below is an allegation made by a member of the public on a platform that publishes complaints without adjudicating them. Circuit Coders takes no position on whether any of it is accurate, and we are not lawyers.

Theme one — the service department

Six of the ten are service or repair issues, and two allege additional damage caused to a vehicle while in the dealership's care. Those are the complaints that escalate if left unanswered, because the customer has nowhere else to go with them.

Theme two — what was on the car versus what was sold

  • A June 2026 buyer of a new CT5-V states the electronic file showed 309 miles on a vehicle sold as new, and describes raising it with the finance office.
  • A September 2025 buyer states they test drove a vehicle with certain features, asked for a different colour, and were told the replacement had the same features — alleging it did not.

Both are documentary. Both are answerable from your own records in minutes. Both are unanswered.

Theme three — F&I terms after signing

  • A July 2025 complainant describes a 10,000-mile lease on a loaner vehicle and a dispute over who is responsible for the miles already on it.
  • A March 2025 complainant describes a vehicle listed online at $15,977 and a materially different figure at the desk.
  • A May 2025 complainant describes agreeing to cosign and disputing what the finance manager presented.

We are not adjudicating these. But three independent complainants describing the gap between the advertised or agreed number and the finance-office number is a disclosure-timing signal, and it is the one category review management genuinely cannot fix. We would rather say that than sell around it.

Theme four — the small stuff that becomes the big stuff

A July 2025 complainant simply wanted a second key after a June purchase and describes being told repeatedly they would be contacted. A complaint about a key is not a reputation problem. A complaint about a key that goes unanswered for a year is.

5What Is Genuinely Well Run

Credit where it is earned.

A store with real problems on the floor does not hold 4.4 stars across 4,137 reviews.

  • 4,137 Google reviews at 4.4 is a large base for a single luxury rooftop, and it is the raw material any recovery runs on.
  • Your service centre outperforms the store at 4.6 on 842 reviews — notable given that service is where the complaints cluster. Both things are true: most service visits go well, and the ones that do not have had nowhere to land.
  • The review pipeline clearly works. Nothing produces 4,137 reviews by accident. Our entire proposal is to point the machine you already built at the platforms it is not pointed at.
  • You are part of a group, which means the fixes here are reusable rather than one-off.

7The Program

Everything below is work we do, not advice we hand you.

Recovery sprint — the first 30 days

  • Claim the Yelp listing. First action, day one. It is unclaimed, it is free, and until it is done nobody at the store can respond to anything there.
  • Put a named person on the BBB record. Replace "Complaint Handler" with a real contact and confirm where notices actually land — this is very likely the root cause of the eighteen-month silence.
  • Answer the BBB backlog. All 12 unanswered complaints drafted, reviewed by you, filed. Where a complaint is resolvable we pursue the resolution, not just the reply.
  • Pull the records on the two documentary ones first — the 309-mile CT5-V and the second-key complaint. Both are answerable from your own system today, and both are the kind that escalate when ignored.
  • Open Yelp properly once claimed: response templates for the existing 42, then every new review answered inside 48 hours.
  • NAP reconciliation — one canonical public number across Google, Yelp, BBB, KBB, Cars.com, DealerRater, CARFAX, Apple and Bing.

Ongoing, monthly

  • Every review on every platform answered within 48 hours, in your voice, signed by a real person.
  • A review pipeline pointed at the unmanaged platforms — the fix for Yelp is not arguing with the reviews you have, it is giving the next several hundred satisfied buyers somewhere to go.
  • BBB complaint monitoring with a named owner, so the grade never regenerates from silence.
  • Listing integrity monitoring — new duplicates, drifting hours, changed numbers.
  • Monthly written report: what moved, what did not, what we recommend next. Plain English.

11Method

So you can check every number rather than trust it.

  • Discovery. BBB's public search API, filtered to F-rated new- and used-car dealers across 54 U.S. metros. 3,368 low-rated dealerships returned; 1,128 franchise rooftops after removing national chains and manufacturer corporate records.
  • BBB. Public profile and complaints pages, read in full. Rating reasons, complaint status counts, business details and contacts quoted verbatim.
  • Google. Business Profile and Maps listing read directly, including the review summary panel, owner-response behaviour and the local competitive set.
  • Yelp. Live listing, first page of reviews read in full, owner-response state and suppressed-review count taken from the page itself.
  • Identity check. The Google listing, service-centre listing, Yelp page, BBB record and Houston Automobile Dealers Association directory entry were compared on name, street address and phone to confirm they describe one business.
  • No access, no scraping of private systems, no contact with your staff or customers. Everything here was readable by anyone with a browser.
