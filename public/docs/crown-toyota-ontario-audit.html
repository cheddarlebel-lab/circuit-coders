Brand & Reputation Audit

Crown Toyota — 1201 Kettering Drive, Ontario, California

Prepared by Leo Lebel, Circuit Coders · Swept live July 30, 2026 · Independent audit, no access required · Twelve surfaces read, every figure re-verifiable · Second pass. Supersedes the July 29 draft, which reached the opposite conclusion on incomplete data.

Executive Summary

The July 29 pass concluded that Crown Toyota's digital presence was "genuinely clean" and that there was no engagement worth selling. It reached that conclusion having read three platforms out of the twelve that matter, and having explicitly skipped the one carrying the store's public reputation.

The full read says something different. The store is well run and its Google reputation is excellent — 4.6 on 9,573 reviews. But it carries an F from the Better Business Bureau, a 3.1 on Yelp, a duplicate unclaimed Yelp listing that is actively overwriting its name on other search engines, four different phone numbers across the review ecosystem, two different street addresses, and a listing on DealerRater that has carried the previous owner's name for three and a half years.

None of that is invented and none of it needed access to find. It is also not unique to Ontario: the same Yelp-versus-Google gap appears at eight of eight Swickard rooftops measured. That moves this from a single-store opportunity to a group-level one — which changes the recommendation, but not the caution about who is able to sign.

3The Finding — Yelp Is 1.5 Stars Wrong, and It Is the One That Travels

2,093 Yelp reviews at 3.1 are setting the public reputation of a store that 9,573 Google reviewers rate 4.6.

Yelp holds 18% of this store's review volume and is 1.5 stars below the platform holding the other 82%. Every transaction-tied platform — Cars.com 5.0, DealerRater 4.9, KBB 4.8, Google 4.6 — agrees the store is good. Yelp is the lone dissenter, and it is the one Bing reprints.

Yelp's own suppression makes the number worse rather than better. Behind the 2,093 shown reviews sit 500 "not currently recommended" reviews — 19% of the total base, filtered out of the score by Yelp's software — plus 80 more removed for terms-of-service violations. The filter disproportionately buries low-activity accounts, which is exactly what someone who buys one car every six years looks like.

Worth crediting, because it changes the pitch: this store already responds on Yelp. Owner replies signed "Swickard A." appear on reviews as recent as July 21, 2026, and they are decent replies — specific, non-defensive. So the 3.1 is not the product of neglect. It is the product of an unmanaged review pipeline: nobody is routing satisfied buyers to Yelp, so only the aggrieved arrive there unprompted. That is a fixable operations problem, not a reputation problem.

4The BBB F

Hard defect
Crown Toyota holds an F from the Better Business Bureau.

BBB's stated reasons: "Failure to respond to 3 complaint(s) filed against business" and 10 complaints filed. Not BBB accredited. The profile lists Swickard Auto Group as an alternate business name, so the F attaches to the group's identity too.

This is the cheapest fix in the document and the most embarrassing to leave alone. Three unanswered complaints is not a quality problem — it is an inbox nobody owns. BBB republishes the failure-to-respond line verbatim on a page that ranks for the store's own name.

5Identity Drift — The Store Has Three Names

Swickard bought this store in January 2023. Three and a half years later the market still has not been told, and the platforms disagree about what the business is called:

  • "John Elway's Crown Toyota" — still the live title on DealerRater, across 1,832 reviews.
  • "Toyota crown" — the unclaimed duplicate Yelp listing at the same street address.
  • "Crown Toyota" — the actual name, on the website, Google and Facebook.

A stale Facebook page for "Steve Khaled — John Elway Crown Toyota" is still live as well.

This is not cosmetic — it is already propagating

Search Crown Toyota on DuckDuckGo and the business comes back titled "Toyota crown", sourced from Yelp. The unclaimed duplicate is not sitting inert; it is the record other engines are choosing. And Google's own "People also ask" for this store leads with "Does John Elway still own Crown Toyota?" — the confusion is measurable in Google's own query data.

9What Is Genuinely Well Run

Credit where it is due — this is not a broken dealership

Google 4.6 on 9,573 reviews with owner responses. Facebook at 86% recommend, properly managed by Swickard Corporation. Cars.com 5.0, DealerRater 4.9, KBB 4.8. Correct title tag, correct meta description, correct and complete AutoDealer schema. Active Instagram. Whoever runs the website and the Google profile is doing it well.

That matters for how this is sold. The pitch here is not "your reputation is bad" — it demonstrably is not. The pitch is that a well-run store is being misrepresented on the three surfaces nobody owns: Yelp, BBB, and the syndicated listings.

10Recommendation — Reverse of the July 29 Draft

There is a real engagement here. It is not a single-rooftop sale, and it should not be pitched at Ontario.

The July 29 conclusion — do not pitch — was right about the buyer and wrong about the findings. Both halves matter. At a 50-rooftop group, brand standards and vendor selection sit at corporate; a proposal walked into the Ontario store has no one inside the building who can sign it. But the finding is real, specific, verifiable, and repeats at every rooftop tested, which is precisely what makes it a group conversation rather than a store one.

The one-sentence version to open with

"Across your Thousand Oaks cluster and Ontario, Yelp sits an average of 1.2 stars below Google on every rooftop we measured — eight for eight — and Bing is reprinting the Yelp number, not yours. 11Method

Read live on 2026-07-30 from public listings only — no client access, no credentials, nothing requested from the dealership. Google and Yelp were read in a real browser because both block automated fetchers; BBB via its public search API and profile; Facebook, KBB, Bing and DuckDuckGo read directly; Cars.com, DealerRater and CARFAX fetched. On-site findings come from the rendered homepage HTML and its JSON-LD blocks. Swickard's rooftop count and California roster are from Swickard's own site.

Two figures are deliberately not asserted: Apple Maps was read through the DuckDuckGo surface rather than Apple's own app, and Edmunds returned 403 to every attempt. Both are open items rather than clean results, and neither changes the finding.

Ratings move. Re-verify every number in this document before it goes in front of anyone outside Circuit Coders.
