Executive Summary

This audit was not commissioned. We ran it because a national sweep of low-rated franchise dealerships across 54 metros returned Route 1 Hyundai. Then we read the detail, and the honest conclusion is narrower — and better for you — than the F suggests.

You are not the store we usually find at the bottom of this list. Most of the dealerships in this sweep ignore BBB entirely; several have gone twelve or eighteen consecutive months without answering a single published complaint. You have not. Of your ten most recent complaints, four are marked Resolved or Answered, including three carried all the way to Resolved with the customer confirming satisfaction. Somebody there does this work.

Your Google presence says the same thing. 4.4 stars on 5,316 reviews, and the owner responses are specific and personal — we read several, including a careful reply to a long critical review about a service wait. That is a real habit, not a template.

So the finding here is a single unattended platform. Yelp rates the same store 1.9 on 107 reviews, with 18 more suppressed by their filter. That is 2.5 stars below your Google rating, describing the same building and the same people. It is the widest gap-to-effort mismatch in this entire campaign: you are clearly willing to do the work, and this one platform has never received any of it.

One theme in the complaints sits outside review management, and we would rather flag it than sell around it: the gap between an advertised price and the final number, including one complaint that names your own website's "Today's Price." Section 3 covers it plainly.

2Yelp — 2.5 Stars Below Everything Else You Do

Highest-leverage defect
107 Yelp reviewers at 1.9 stars are setting the first impression of a store that 5,316 Google reviewers rate 4.4.

Yelp carries about 2% of your total review volume and sits 2.5 stars below the platform carrying the rest.

What makes this unusually fixable in your case:

  • The page is already claimed. Somebody at the store has the login. No recovery process, no waiting on Yelp support.
  • You already know how to do the work. This is the part that separates you from most stores in this sweep. Your Google replies are specific, signed and non-defensive — one recent response to a detailed complaint about a long service appointment acknowledges the wait and the horn issue directly. That exact voice, pointed at Yelp, is the entire fix.
  • 18 reviews are sitting suppressed. Yelp's filter has moved them into "not currently recommended," where they do not count toward the score. That filter disproportionately buries reviews from low-activity accounts — which is exactly what somebody who buys one car every six years looks like.

Worth noting in your favour: your Google review tags include "leasing process" (342 mentions) and "great deal" (115), and one reviewer writes "shout out to the owner who is also hands on with the customers." That is not a store with a reputation problem. It is a store with an unattended page.

The theme worth your attention — advertised price versus final price

Three separate complainants, across two years:

  • A December 2025 buyer of a used 2023 Acura RDX states they relied on the "Today's Price" of $34,061 listed on the dealership's own website, and describes what followed. This one is unanswered. It is also the most specific: it names your own advertised figure, on your own site, on a stated date.
  • An October 2025 buyer of a certified Palisade describes fees discovered during the process rather than disclosed up front. Also unanswered.
  • An October 2024 buyer describes a $1,950 "used car inspection" charge and asking what it was. That one you carried to Resolved — which is exactly the right outcome and proves the process works when it is engaged.

The consistent shape is a disclosure-timing question: the charge exists somewhere, and buyers are meeting it in the finance office rather than in the advertisement. Moving one disclosure line from the footer into the offer is close to free, and it retires an entire complaint category. Review management does not touch it, and we would rather say so.

Secondary theme — service diagnosis and follow-through

A May 2026 complainant describes an emissions-recall repair, a check-engine light returning within ten minutes, and being told it was a phantom code. Marked Unresolved. Two earlier service complaints from 2024 were carried to Resolved, including one where the store offered a full refund after six months of failed diagnosis.

The pattern in your own complaint history is the argument for doing this.

Where you engaged, complaints closed Resolved. Where you did not, they sit public and permanent. That is a process gap, not a capability gap — and it is the cheapest kind to close.

7The Program

Everything below is work we do, not advice we hand you.

Recovery sprint — the first 30 days

  • Open Yelp first. The page is claimed, so this starts day one: response templates for the existing 107, then every new review answered inside 48 hours — in the voice your Google replies already use.
  • Clear the small BBB backlog. Two unanswered complaints and four unresolved. This is an afternoon, not a project, and it moves the letter grade.
  • Answer the December 2025 pricing complaint specifically. It names your own website's advertised figure on a stated date, which means it is answerable from your own records — and it is the one most likely to be read by another shopper.
  • Attack the suppressed pile. 18 filtered reviews re-weight as legitimate accounts gain activity; the fix is volume from real buyers, not argument.
  • NAP reconciliation — one canonical public number across Google, Yelp, BBB, KBB, Cars.com, DealerRater, CARFAX, Apple and Bing, with the Genesis listing kept cleanly separate.
  • Disclosure review on the advertised-price theme in Section 3. We mark up the current language and hand you a revised version. You decide whether to adopt it.

Ongoing, monthly

  • Every review on every platform answered within 48 hours, in your voice, signed by a real person.
  • A review pipeline pointed at the unmanaged platforms — the fix for Yelp is not arguing with the reviews you have, it is giving the next several hundred satisfied buyers somewhere to go.
  • BBB complaint monitoring with a named owner, so the grade never regenerates from silence.
  • Listing integrity monitoring — new duplicates, drifting hours, changed numbers.
  • Monthly written report: what moved, what did not, what we recommend next. Plain English.

10Method

So you can check every number rather than trust it.

  • Discovery. BBB's public search API, filtered to F-rated new- and used-car dealers across 54 U.S. metros. 3,368 low-rated dealerships returned; 1,128 franchise rooftops after removing national chains and manufacturer corporate records.
  • BBB. Public profile and complaints pages, read in full. Rating reasons, complaint status counts, business details and contacts quoted verbatim.
  • Google. Business Profile and Maps listing read directly, including the review summary panel, owner-response behaviour and the local competitive set.
  • Yelp. Live listing, first page of reviews read in full, owner-response state and suppressed-review count taken from the page itself.
  • No access, no scraping of private systems, no contact with your staff or customers. Everything here was readable by anyone with a browser.
