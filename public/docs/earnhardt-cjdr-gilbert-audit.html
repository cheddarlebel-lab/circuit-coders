Executive Summary

This audit was not commissioned. We ran it because a national sweep of low-rated franchise dealerships across 54 metros returned this store. Then we read the file, and the honest conclusion is narrower — and considerably better for you — than an F implies.

Your Google rating is the highest of any dealership in this entire sweep. 4.7 stars on 7,593 reviews. We measured 3,368 low-rated dealerships nationwide and nothing else came close to that combination of rating and volume.

And you actually work your BBB profile. Of the ten most recent complaints BBB publishes, nine are Answered or Resolved. Most stores in this sweep have gone twelve to eighteen months without answering anything. You are not that store, and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

So there are exactly two findings, and neither is what the letter grade suggests.

One — every single complaint is service. All ten. Not one originates in sales, F&I or delivery. That is unusual and it is diagnostically useful: it means the problem has a department, not a culture. Section 3 lays out what the ten actually say.

Two — Yelp sits 1.9 stars below Google, and 229 of your reviews there are suppressed. Yelp shows 2.8 on 625 reviews with another 229 filtered out of the score entirely — one of the largest suppressed piles we have seen. That is a platform carrying real volume and getting none of the attention the rest of your operation gets.

Your F is not a response-rate problem, and that makes it unusual.

Almost every F we investigated in this sweep came down to silence — ten, fourteen, twenty-one complaints with no reply at all. Yours does not. You answer. The grade is being driven by five complaints that closed Unresolved, meaning BBB judged the response didn't satisfy the complainant, plus four that slipped through unanswered.

That is a materially different problem, and it needs a different fix: better resolutions, not more replies.

The 229 suppressed Yelp reviews deserve their own sentence. That is more than a third of your total Yelp volume sitting in "not currently recommended," invisible to the score. On a base of 625, moving even part of that pile changes the number a shopper sees.

3What the Complaints Actually Say — All Ten Are Service

We read the published texts rather than just the counts, and the pattern is the clearest we have seen at any store in this sweep.

The diagnostic finding
All ten published complaints are Service or Repair Issues. Zero from sales, F&I, trade-in or delivery.

At most dealerships this file is a mix — advertised-vs-final price, add-on products, paperwork delays. Yours has none of that. Whatever is happening, it is happening in one department.

Stated plainly before anything else.

Everything below is an allegation made by a member of the public on a platform that publishes complaints without adjudicating them. Circuit Coders takes no position on whether any of it is accurate, and we are not lawyers.

The dominant theme — repair duration and repeat visits

  • A January 2025 complainant describes a Jeep Gladiator brought in November 14, 2024 for a minor repair, followed by repeated delays.
  • A September 2025 complainant states they have been bringing a vehicle in every few months for repeated and ignored issues, with new problems accumulating.
  • An April 2026 complainant describes a 2025 SUV with major electrical failures and power loss on highways, and difficulty reaching Service.

Theme two — warranty scope disputes

  • A September 2025 complainant describes an independent shop identifying a cooler-hose leak and contacting you to confirm warranty coverage. Resolved.
  • An August 2024 complainant describes a 2012 Wrangler engine replaced in March 2021, and a dispute over whether the replacement motor was genuinely new and what warranty applied.
  • An October 2024 complainant describes four tires purchased with warranties and a replacement that went sideways.

The one we would read first

A June 2025 complainant identifies themselves as a military retiree, deployed overseas, now retired, describing themselves as devastated by an experience at the repair shop. That one is marked Answered — but it is the sort of narrative that, left visible and unresolved on a public page, does disproportionate damage regardless of the underlying facts.

Why this is good news, oddly.

A complaint file that is 100% one department is far easier to fix than one spread across the store. Five of these closed Unresolved — that is a resolution-quality problem inside service, and it is addressable with a process rather than a culture change.

6The Program

Everything below is work we do, not advice we hand you.

Recovery sprint — the first 30 days

  • Open Yelp properly. 625 reviews and 229 suppressed is the largest single opportunity here. Response templates for the existing base, then every new review answered inside 48 hours.
  • Work the suppressed pile. A review pipeline pointed at Yelp specifically — 229 filtered reviews re-weight as accounts gain activity, and on a 625 base that materially moves the visible score.
  • Convert Unresolved to Resolved. Five complaints closed unresolved is what is actually driving your grade. We re-open each with you, pursue the resolution rather than the reply, and file the update.
  • Answer the four unanswered. Small backlog, straightforward.
  • Service-department complaint routing. Since all ten complaints originate in one department, we set up a monitored intake so a repeat-visit pattern surfaces before it reaches BBB.
  • NAP reconciliation across Google, Yelp, BBB, KBB, Cars.com, DealerRater, CARFAX, Apple and Bing.

Ongoing, monthly

  • Every review on every platform answered within 48 hours, in your voice, signed by a real person.
  • A review pipeline pointed at the unmanaged platforms — the fix for Yelp is not arguing with the reviews you have, it is giving the next several hundred satisfied buyers somewhere to go.
  • BBB complaint monitoring with a named owner, so the grade never regenerates from silence.
  • Listing integrity monitoring — new duplicates, drifting hours, changed numbers.
  • Monthly written report: what moved, what did not, what we recommend next. Plain English.

9Method

So you can check every number rather than trust it.

  • Discovery. BBB's public search API, filtered to F-rated new- and used-car dealers across 54 U.S. metros. 3,368 low-rated dealerships returned; 1,128 franchise rooftops after removing national chains and manufacturer corporate records.
  • BBB. Public profile and complaints pages, read in full. Rating reasons, complaint status counts, business details and contacts quoted verbatim.
  • Google. Business Profile and Maps listing read directly, including the review summary panel, owner-response behaviour and the local competitive set.
  • Yelp. Live listing, first page of reviews read in full, owner-response state and suppressed-review count taken from the page itself.
  • No access, no scraping of private systems, no contact with your staff or customers. Everything here was readable by anyone with a browser.
