Executive Summary

This audit was not commissioned. We ran it because a national sweep of low-rated franchise dealerships surfaced Daytona Kia at the very bottom, and the reason turned out to be more interesting than the ranking.

Your store is not badly rated. It is unevenly rated, and the split is almost perfectly clean. On every platform where somebody at the dealership is actively working — Google, Cars.com, DealerRater, Kelley Blue Book, CARFAX — Daytona Kia sits between 4.4 and 4.5 stars across more than 15,000 reviews. On Google specifically you are 4.5 on 4,515 reviews, the highest rating in your local dealer set, and someone on your team replies to reviews within days, by name. That is genuinely good work and it is the reason this document is worth reading: it proves the store can already do this.

On the platforms where nobody is working, the picture inverts. Yelp is 2.2 stars. The Better Business Bureau grades you F — the lowest score of 2,820 low-rated dealerships we measured nationwide — for one stated reason: 16 complaints were never answered. Facebook sits at 3.7. All three are free to work on, none require a vendor's permission, and the Yelp page is already claimed under your name.

The cost of the split is that the unmanaged surfaces are the ones a stranger hits first. Search your own store name on Google and Yelp is the third result — above your Facebook page, above MapQuest, above everything except your own website and CARFAX. The 2.2 is what greets a shopper checking whether you are legitimate, while the 4.5 sits on a platform they reach only after they have already decided to trust you.

One finding sits outside the reputation frame and we would rather say it than sell around it. The complaints that drive the BBB grade are not scattered. They cluster on two specific, upstream things — a direct-mail sweepstakes program and the gap between an advertised price and the final number — and the same two allegations appear independently on Yelp, from different people, in different years. Review management will not fix that. Section 4 covers it plainly, because a vendor who sold you review-generation and stayed quiet about it would be taking your money under false pretenses.

2Yelp — 2.2 Stars, Claimed, and Third on Your Own Name

Highest-leverage defect
121 Yelp reviewers at 2.2 stars are setting the first impression of a store that 4,515 Google reviewers rate 4.5.

Yelp carries under 1% of your total review volume and sits 2.3 stars below the platform carrying the rest. It is also the third organic result when someone searches "Daytona Kia" — we ran it.

Three things make this the cheapest fix in the document:

  • The page is already claimed. Yelp lists the business owner as "Richard N." Somebody at the store has the login. No recovery process, no waiting on Yelp support.
  • Not one review has an owner response. We read the full first page — reviews from July 2026 back to February 2025, one and five star alike. Every one is unanswered. On Google you reply to nearly everything, often within a day. The capability exists; it has simply never been pointed at this platform.
  • 60 reviews are sitting suppressed. Yelp's filter has moved 60 reviews — roughly a third of your total base there — into "not currently recommended," where they do not count toward the score. That filter disproportionately buries reviews from low-activity accounts, which is exactly what somebody who buys one car every six years looks like.

Worth noting in your favour: the recent Yelp reviews are not uniformly hostile. A July 8, 2026 reviewer describes "a little bump in the road which they made good on" and names a salesperson positively. The platform is recoverable — it has just never been worked.

3The BBB F — One Sentence Explains It

Hard defect
Daytona Kia holds an F from the Better Business Bureau, and BBB publishes the reason on a page that ranks for your name.

BBB's two stated reasons, verbatim: "Failure to respond to 16 complaint(s) filed against business" and "6 complaint(s) filed against business that were not resolved." The business is not BBB accredited.

The complaint ledger, as BBB publishes it — 29 complaints over three years, 12 closed in the last twelve months:

StatusCountWhat it means, in BBB's own words
Unanswered16"The business failed to respond to the dispute."
Unresolved6Business responded but "failed to make a good faith effort to resolve it."
Answered6Addressed, but the consumer did not confirm satisfaction.
Resolved1Complainant verified the issue was fixed.
Total29One of twenty-nine closed to the complainant's satisfaction.

This is the single most fixable item here, and the most expensive to leave alone. BBB's algorithm punishes silence far more heavily than it punishes the underlying complaint — a business that answers every complaint and resolves none still outranks one that ignores sixteen. Sixteen unanswered disputes is not a quality failure. It is an inbox nobody owns.

It also compounds. BBB routes complaint notices to the contact on file — listed publicly as Mike Tacinelli, Sales Manager. If that routing has drifted, or lands somewhere nobody checks, the F will keep regenerating no matter how well the store performs on the floor.

4What the Complaints Actually Say

We read the published complaint texts rather than just the counts, because the counts alone would have led us to sell you the wrong thing.

Stated plainly before anything else in this section.

Everything below is an allegation made by a member of the public on a platform that publishes complaints without adjudicating them. Circuit Coders takes no position on whether any of it is accurate, and we are not lawyers. We are flagging it because these are the words currently sitting on pages that rank for your store's name — and because two themes repeat across independent complainants, on two different platforms, in different years. That pattern is worth your attention regardless of the merits of any single complaint.

Theme one — the sweepstakes mailer

A BBB complaint (status: unanswered) describes a mailed sweepstakes with a phone number to confirm a winning number, a recorded confirmation of a win, and then no prize at the store. An unrelated Yelp reviewer in August 2025 describes the same sequence in almost the same terms and states they filed with the Florida Attorney General's Office and the U.S. Postal Service.

Two strangers, two platforms, a year apart, describing one marketing program. Whatever the program's actual terms are, its disclosures are not landing — and it is generating regulatory complaints rather than showroom traffic. That is a campaign problem with a compliance tail, and no amount of review management touches it.

Theme two — advertised price versus final price

Three separate complainants, independently:

  • A BBB complaint alleging a vehicle purchased for over $26,000, followed the next morning by a dealership email advertising the same vehicle at $17,915.
  • A BBB complaint alleging a vehicle advertised at $11,500 presented at $21,900, the difference attributed to a trade-in requirement the complainant says was not disclosed up front. Their words: "hidden at the end of their website and not easily visible."
  • An April 2026 Yelp reviewer describing market-adjustment addenda of $6,000–$10,000 over sticker attached to a lease-payoff promotion.

Again — we are not adjudicating these. But the consistent shape is a disclosure-timing problem: the qualifying condition exists somewhere, and buyers are meeting it in the finance office instead of in the ad. Moving one disclosure line from the footer to the offer is close to free, and it retires an entire complaint category.

Theme three — the phones

Google's own review summary for your store surfaces this line among its top quotes:

"Get recording saying mail box is full for sales finance and reception desk."

A full voicemail box on sales, finance, and reception is a direct revenue leak, and it is a fifteen-minute fix. We flag it because it is the kind of thing that never reaches an owner — the people who hit it do not call back to tell you.

5The Dead Domain BBB Points At

Verified defect
The "Visit Website" button on your BBB profile links to a domain that no longer resolves.

BBB lists daytonamitsubishi.com as the primary website for the Daytona Kia profile. That domain currently has no DNS A record at all — it does not fail to load, it fails to exist. Your real Kia site, daytonakia.com, is demoted to "Additional Websites" further down the page.

The domain is still registered and its nameservers still point at dealer.com — a previous website vendor. Your current site is built by Team Velocity. So the most likely history is straightforward: the platform was switched, the old domain was left behind, and the BBB record was never updated to follow. Meanwhile the sister store's live site is at daytonamitsu.com, a third address again.

Net effect: a visitor who lands on your F-rated BBB profile and clicks the one button offered to hear your side of it reaches nothing at all. Both halves of that are fixable this week.

7What Is Genuinely Well Run

Credit where it is earned — and the reason this is worth doing.

A store with real problems on the floor does not hold 4.5 stars across 15,000+ reviews. The operation is working. What follows is a list of things we would not change.

  • Google review response is excellent. Replies are specific, personal, signed, and land within days. Several name the employee the customer praised. This is the hardest habit to build in a dealership and you already have it.
  • Review volume is a genuine asset. 4,515 on Google, 5,373 on Cars.com, 4,873 on DealerRater. Very few single rooftops carry that.
  • Your Google rating leads your market. Against the local set: Daytona Kia 4.5 (4,515) · Daytona Nissan 4.5 (4,323) · Daytona Toyota 4.4 (4,095) · Deland Kia 4.4 (3,694) · Daytona Hyundai 4.3 (1,501). You are first on rating and first on volume among Kia's local competition.
  • The website surfaces reviews honestly. The homepage carries a live Google feed rather than a curated testimonial slider. That is the right instinct.
  • Named staff are individually well rated on DealerRater, including the dealer principal. Reviewers repeatedly name specific people. That is the raw material a review pipeline runs on.
  • 26 years in business under a corporation formed in 1990. Longevity is the one thing no competitor can buy.

13Method

So you can check every number rather than trust it.

  • Discovery. BBB's public search API, filtered to D and F rated new- and used-car dealers across 46 U.S. metros. 2,820 low-rated dealerships returned; 1,223 franchise rooftops after removing national chains. Daytona Kia carried the lowest BBB score in the set.
  • BBB. Public profile and complaints pages, read in full. Rating reasons, complaint status counts, business details and contacts quoted verbatim.
  • Google. Business Profile and Maps listing read directly, including the review summary panel and the local competitive set. Branded SERP position checked on a clean query.
  • Yelp. Live listing, first page of reviews read in full, owner-response state and suppressed-review count taken from the page itself.
  • Cars.com, DealerRater, KBB, CARFAX, Facebook, Trustpilot. Ratings and review counts as published on July 30, 2026.
  • Technical. DNS resolution and WHOIS on all four Daytona-branded domains; HTTP status on each; site platform identified from page markup.
  • No access, no scraping of private systems, no contact with your staff or customers. Everything here was readable by anyone with a browser.
