# What Needs to Change as a Business — Toyota & Kia of Vero Beach
*Circuit Coders · 2026-06-22 · drawn from verified public review/complaint patterns (BBB, DealerRater, CARFAX, Cars.com, Yelp, Google). This is the operational layer no marketing can fix — the processes that generate the complaints. Internal-first; the client-facing version gets softened and runs through Leo before it reaches Jared.*

## The core finding
The stores are **liked** — individual salespeople and advisors are praised *by name* across every platform. The recurring negative reviews are almost never about a person; they're about **two repeatable process failures**. Marketing can amplify the good and respond to the bad, but the rating ceiling won't lift until these two processes change. That's the honest message to Jared.

## 1. Finance office — terms/add-ons surprise (the #1 theme)
**Pattern across BBB + DealerRater + Cars.com:** financing terms changed after a verbal agreement, add-on products (GAP, VSC, tire & wheel) appearing without a clear opt-in, and slow GAP/warranty refund processing.
**What to change:**
- **Put the numbers in writing before F&I.** A one-page "out-the-door" summary the customer sees and initials *before* the finance office, so nothing "changes" at signing.
- **Make every add-on an explicit opt-in line**, presented as optional with a plain "no thanks" path — not a default the customer has to catch.
- **Set a refund SLA** (e.g. GAP/warranty cancellations processed within X days) and track it — slow refunds are a recurring, avoidable complaint.
> Why it matters: this is also the exact gap our website copy now *promises* to close ("ask for your total in writing"). If the floor doesn't actually do it, the pages manufacture new complaints. The process change and the marketing have to move together.

## 2. Service pricing — quote vs. counter mismatch
**Pattern across Google + Cars.com + Yelp (esp. Kia):** "didn't know what I'd pay until checkout," phone quote not matching the counter, the "alignment at 15k miles" upsell story repeated near-verbatim.
**What to change:**
- **Publish a starting-price menu** for common services and honor it (the website is built to surface this — but only if the counter matches).
- **Written estimate before work begins**, and **photo/video approval for anything added** mid-service (most DMS/MPI tools already support this) — customer approves from their phone, nothing added silently.
- **Align phone and counter quoting** so the BDC/service-line number matches the in-person price.

## 3. Respond to reviews — the cheapest fix, currently zero
Across the review platforms (separate from BBB, where complaints *were* answered), **negative reviews sit unanswered.** Shoppers read the *response*, not just the star. A consistent 24-hour, non-defensive reply changes how the whole profile reads even before ratings move. (This is what our review engine now delivers — but it depends on the two process fixes above to have something good to say.)

## 4. Structural reputation hygiene (we handle these; flagged so Jared sees the scope)
- BBB **F** = a licensing *records gap* (active MV4029) → correctable, in motion.
- NAP fragmentation (5+ phone numbers per store, wrong addresses/ZIPs on listings) — confusing customers and suppressing map rank.
- Duplicate FB/Yelp listings splitting the reputation; Kia's CARFAX/Yelp surfaces weak.

## The one-line version for Jared
> "Your people are great — the reviews say so by name. The two things costing you stars are the finance-office surprise and the service-price surprise. Fix how those are *presented* (everything in writing, add-ons opt-in, nothing added without approval), and we'll make the brand around it tell the truth. We can't out-market a process the reviews keep catching."
