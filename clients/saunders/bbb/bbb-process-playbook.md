# BBB Process Playbook — Saunders Dealerships (Toyota / Kia of Vero Beach)
Research date: 2026-06-12. Local bureau: BBB Serving Southeast Florida & the Caribbean (handles Vero Beach / Indian River County, prefix 0633 on profile IDs).

## (a) Responding to old complaints

- Complaints stay on the profile for **3 years from filing date**, then drop off automatically. Toyota's 09/25/2023 and 10/04/2023 complaints age off in ~3–4 months; Kia's 07/19/2023 next month.
- Standard flow: BBB forwards a complaint within 2 business days; business has **14 days** to respond (one second request if missed); complaints generally close within 30 days. Non-response directly damages the rating.
- Both stores have **responded to every visible complaint** — good. For old complaints already closed as "Answered," a business can still submit an updated/supplemental response through the BBB business portal (bluebbb.org / the business login emailed with each complaint) or by contacting the local bureau's dispute-resolution team. The complaint stays on the record either way; the visible text and status can improve ("Answered" → "Resolved" only if the consumer confirms satisfaction).
- Practical play: for open/rejected recent complaints, deliver the actual remedy off-platform, then ask the customer to tell BBB they're satisfied — that's the only path to "Resolved" status. The drafts in response-drafts.md are built for this.
- Complaints cannot be removed for being unflattering. Removal only happens for BBB process violations (wrong business, abusive content, non-marketplace dispute) — none apply here.

## (b) Disputing/correcting the licensing alert

**The facts:** BBB's Toyota profile shows "Failure to have a required competency license." The dealership in fact holds **active Florida motor vehicle dealer license MV4029, valid through 9/16/2027**. This is a BBB *records gap*, not a licensing problem. (Internal language discipline: never say or write "unlicensed" — including in BBB correspondence; frame it as "BBB's file does not yet reflect the dealership's active license.")

**How BBB scores it:** Competency licensing is a 0 / -41 element in BBB's published rating algorithm — compliance earns nothing; a missing-license flag costs **41 points**, which alone is roughly the distance from A+ to F territory. This single deduction is the dominant cause of the F.

**Correction process:**
1. Gather proof: license detail printout/screenshot from the Florida DHSMV/FLHSMV dealer-license lookup (MV4029, status Active, expiry 9/16/2027) and/or the physical license certificate.
2. Contact BBB Serving Southeast Florida & the Caribbean (West Palm Beach office) — request a **business profile update / licensing verification review**. Channels: the business-update form on the profile ("Update Business Information"), the BBB business portal, or phone to the local bureau. Put MV4029, issuing agency, and expiry in writing.
3. BBB verifies against the state database and updates the file. Once the license is on file, the -41 deduction is removed and the alert comes down. There is no fee and no accreditation requirement to correct factual profile data.
4. Also verify the Kia store's license is on BBB's file (Kia is named on the Toyota profile; the alert may be tied to the combined record — ask BBB whether the two storefronts should have properly separated license records).

**This is the single highest-leverage action in the entire engagement.**

## (c) What moves an F upward, and timelines

BBB's 13-element algorithm (per BBB's published ratings overview): complaint volume relative to business size, unanswered complaints, unresolved complaints, complaint recency (older complaints weigh less), seriousness, time in business, transparency, licensing (-41), advertising-review issues, government actions, and accreditation standing.

Levers, in order of impact:
1. **Fix the licensing record (+41 points).** Expect the rating to recompute within days to a few weeks of BBB verifying. This alone should move F to somewhere in the B/C range given Kia's B+ with similar complaint volume and no alert.
2. **Convert "Answered" to "Resolved"** on recent complaints (customer confirms satisfaction) — reduces the unresolved-complaint deduction.
3. **Keep answering everything within 14 days** — already happening; maintain it.
4. **Let time work.** Complaint weight decays with age and complaints drop off at 3 years. With 10 closed in the last 12 months at Toyota, reducing new-complaint inflow (F&I doc handover at delivery, written service estimates, add-on disclosure sheet) matters more than litigating old ones.

**Realistic timeline:** licensing fix = days–weeks for a major jump; remaining climb to A-range = 6–18 months of clean responsiveness and falling complaint volume. Ratings recalculate continuously as file data changes; there is no annual review to wait for.

## (d) Accreditation — recommendation only (no action)

- **Requirements:** meet the 8 BBB Standards for Trust (build trust, advertise honestly, tell the truth, be transparent, honor promises, be responsive, safeguard privacy, embody integrity); business operational ≥6 months; required licensing in place; **a B rating or better is required** — so Toyota cannot apply until the licensing alert is fixed and the rating recovers. Kia (B+) is technically eligible now.
- **Cost:** application ~$75–$200 admin (varies by bureau) plus annual dues scaled by employee count — typically **~$500–$1,200/yr per location** for businesses of dealership size; multi-rooftop quotes are negotiated with the local bureau. Budget roughly $1–2.5k/yr for both stores.
- **What it buys:** the seal, "Accredited" on the profile, eligibility for an A+ ceiling (non-accredited caps at A), and BBB dispute-resolution support. It does not remove complaints or alter complaint handling.
- **Recommendation:** **Not now.** Sequence: (1) fix licensing record, (2) work the open complaints, (3) reassess in Q4 2026 once Toyota is at B or better. At that point accreditation for both rooftops is cheap brand insurance for a dealership whose buyers actively check BBB — worth it at ~$2k/yr combined, but applying from an F profile is wasted money and a likely rejection. Decision is Jared's; no action taken.
