# Why Indian River County Drivers Are Done Driving to Melbourne and Fort Pierce for a Car

For years, the assumption around here was that buying a car meant a drive. North to Melbourne, south to Fort Pierce — wherever the inventory or the deal seemed to be. You'd make the trip, sign the papers, and then make that same trip again every few months for the next several years for oil changes, tire rotations, and warranty work.

More and more Indian River County drivers are looking at that math and deciding it doesn't add up anymore. The dealerships are right here on US-1. And once you count the *whole* relationship — not just the day you buy, but the years of service that follow — staying local stops being about convenience and starts being about real time and money.

## The real cost of the 40-minute dealership

A round trip to Melbourne or Fort Pierce is roughly 80 minutes of driving, often more with traffic on US-1 or I-95. That's the part everyone underestimates: you don't make that drive once. You make it every time your car needs attention.

Over a typical ownership period, a vehicle sees regular maintenance several times a year — oil and filter, tire rotations, brake checks, the occasional warranty item. Across five-plus years, that's easily **15 to 20 service visits.** Now multiply 80-plus minutes of driving by 15-to-20 trips, and you're looking at the better part of a full day or more spent on the road — plus the fuel, plus the wear, plus arranging a ride home each time because your car's at a shop a county away.

A local dealership doesn't just shorten one trip. It shortens *every* trip, for the entire life of the vehicle. That's the part the sticker price never shows you.

And it's not only the scheduled maintenance. It's the unplanned moments, too — a warning light on a Saturday morning, a slow leak before a road trip, a recall notice in the mail. When your dealer is ten minutes away, those become quick errands instead of half-day expeditions. You can drop the car off on your way to work, walk over to grab coffee, or wait in the lounge and still make your afternoon. When the same situations happen with a dealership a county away, every one of them turns into a logistics problem: who drives the second car, how do you get back, when can you even spare the block of time. Proximity quietly removes a category of stress most people never factor in until they're living it.

## Kia's 10-year/100,000-mile warranty makes proximity compound

Here's where it really matters, and it's specific to Kia. Kia backs its vehicles with one of the longest factory warranties in the industry — **10 years or 100,000 miles** on the powertrain. That's a long relationship by design.

A long warranty is a genuine asset, but only if you actually use the dealer network behind it. Warranty work, recall service, and the documented maintenance that keeps your coverage valid all run through the dealer. Over ten years, that's potentially **around 20 service touchpoints** tied to your warranty. Every one of those is a trip.

So the proximity advantage doesn't just add up — it *compounds.* The longer your warranty, the more visits you'll make, and the more a five-minute drive instead of a forty-minute one is worth. Buying your Kia ten minutes from home isn't a small convenience. Over the warranty's full life, it's measured in days of your time given back.
