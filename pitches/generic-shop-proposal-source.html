Executive Summary

Your shop does genuinely excellent work — quality repairs, real attention to detail, and customers who trust you. That reputation, though, mostly lives in your customers' heads and not where new customers look. Today most new jobs have to come through one channel — the phone, during business hours — and your online presence (reviews, search visibility, lead capture) is doing almost none of the selling it could be doing.

This proposal lays out three options, smallest to largest. Each one is scoped honestly: we describe what we do and what we capture, not outcomes we can't control. You can start at any tier and move up later.

1What We Typically Find

Before anything is signed, we run a quick live audit of your specific shop. But across shops like yours, the same handful of gaps show up again and again — and each one is quietly costing jobs:

1. The phone is the only door in — and it closes at 5

For most shops, every new job starts with a phone call, during business hours. A breakdown or a fender-bender doesn't keep business hours. Tonight's and this weekend's callers reach voicemail — and a voicemail competing with a shop that picks up usually loses the job.

2. Your reputation is nearly invisible online

Plenty of excellent shops have a thin public review footprint — a small handful of reviews across Google and Yelp — while a competitor down the street has dozens or hundreds. New customers comparing shops see their wall of reviews and your near-empty one. The work may be better; the scoreboard says otherwise.

Exact review counts and competitor figures are confirmed live during the audit before signing — review platforms change daily.

3. The website can't capture or convert

  • No online booking or estimate-request form — most shop sites make every call-to-action a "call us" phone link. There's no way for a customer to request an estimate or appointment online, day or night.
  • No live chat to catch the visitor who won't pick up the phone.
  • Weak local-search structured data — many shop sites carry only a bare-bones "WebSite" schema tag, with no LocalBusiness markup for the address, hours, or service area. That structured data is exactly what Google reads to rank you in the local map pack and "near me" results.
  • No paid acquisition — automotive work is high-intent and high-value, yet there's often no paid search running while competitors can bid on your customers.

What's already working (and stays): most shops we work with already have a clean, mobile-friendly site with solid on-page basics — a clear title, hours, and contact info — plus a shop-management system that's the industry standard and genuine repair quality and word-of-mouth. We build on a good foundation, not replace one. The audit confirms exactly what's strong at your shop so we don't touch what's already working.

For perspective: a full-time front-desk receptionist runs $3,000–$4,500/mo, and a single missed job is $1,500–$6,000 in lost work. Recovering even one extra job a month covers the service several times over.

AI Phone Receptionist

An AI voice assistant answers the phone 24/7. It greets callers, answers common questions (hours, location, services, "do you work with insurance?"), collects the caller's name, vehicle, and the details of what they need, and books an estimate or appointment request on your calendar. After hours, it texts the lead straight to you so nothing waits until morning. Bilingual English/Spanish. Every call logged.

What it honestly does: it makes sure every call is answered and every lead is captured. It does not give firm repair prices (an accurate quote requires seeing the vehicle) and it won't catch the rare caller who hangs up on an automated voice — a human still confirms each appointment.

3How This Works Alongside Your Shop-Management System

The system your shop already runs on — for collision shops that's usually CCC ONE; for general and performance shops, Mitchell, Shop-Ware, Tekmetric or similar — is the hub. We build around it. Nothing gets ripped out or replaced.

What your system already does — we configure it, we don't rebuild it

Most shop-management platforms include built-in automated status texts that keep customers updated through a repair. Where that's the right fit, we simply turn it on and tune the messaging — you should never pay us to rebuild a feature you already own.

What we integrate, with your authorization

Many systems expose a secure data connection (for example, CCC's Secure Share network — a cloud API in the industry-standard CIECA BMS format) that lets an approved app read your job data. With your sign-off, we connect to it so that:

  • When a job reaches completion (e.g. Final Bill), that's the automatic trigger for the review-request text and any follow-up — no one at the front desk has to remember.
  • Customer and vehicle details flow in to personalize every message we send.

Where a vendor approval or per-job data fee applies, we handle it. Until that connection is live, every one of these automations runs off a one-tap "job done" at the front desk, so nothing waits on the approval process.

What lives outside your system — this is what we build

Your shop-management system only knows about jobs already written up. Everything upstream of that — the missed call, the tire-kicker, the after-hours lead — is invisible to it, and that's exactly where the lost revenue is. We build the layer it doesn't cover:

  • The AI phone receptionist and online booking form (capture the lead before it's ever a written-up job).
  • The review-request engine and lead follow-up for callers who never became a work order.
  • Your reporting dashboard, tying calls, leads, jobs, and reviews together in one view.
