Phil Smith Automotive Group — all thirteen dealerships

Prepared for Jared Saunders  ·  Prepared by Leo Lebel, Circuit Coders  ·  Audited July 27, 2026

Executive Summary

Thirteen dealerships across Florida and North Carolina, carrying roughly 37,600 Google reviews at an average of 4.55 stars. These are healthy, well-run stores. Their public reputation is being written by a platform holding three percent of that volume.

Yelp rates this group at 2.4 stars. Google rates the same stores at 4.55. That gap holds at every rooftop we measured — eleven for eleven, both states, every brand. It is not one weak store or one difficult market. It is one unmanaged platform, and it is the one that surfaces when a shopper searches your dealership by name.

Alongside it: six duplicate and phantom listings splitting your reviews, a data-breach notice sitting unmonitored in the footer of every store, an unanswered complaint thread on Kia of Vero Beach, and a domain carrying live staff email that expires in October.

Every figure below was read directly from the public listing on July 27, 2026 and is independently re-verifiable. No estimates, no modelled numbers, no third-party data brokers.

What this looks like at one store

Twenty-three people on Yelp give Southern Pines Kia 1.6 stars. Meanwhile 2,077 other customers — 857 on Google, 587 on CARFAX, 633 on Birdeye — rate the same store between 4.5 and 4.6.

Twenty-three reviews are beating two thousand, because Yelp ranks on a brand-name search and the others do not.

There is a mechanical reason for this

Yelp's own listing for Toyota of Vero Beach shows 114 recommended reviews and 110 more that it hides — it suppresses roughly half of everything written about the store. Its filter systematically buries reviews from low-activity accounts, which is exactly what a person who buys one car every six years looks like. Your satisfied customer is filtered out. The prolific complainer is not.

Independent evidence the stores are well run

This matters, because the audit is not a criticism of the operation:

  • CarEdge, which grades dealers on verified out-the-door pricing, awards Grade A to Phil Smith Acura, Southern Pines Kia and Southern Pines Chevrolet. Its verdict on the Acura store: "One of the good ones. You can walk in here with confidence."
  • CARFAX, tied to verified service transactions, rates every store measured between 4.2 and 4.7.
  • Mercedes-Benz of Fayetteville holds the Best of the Best award from Mercedes-Benz, two years running.

2What Else We Found

1. A data-breach notice is live in the footer of every store

philsmithauto.com/security-notice.htm carries a Notice of Data Incident. In the group's own words:

"…posting this substitute notice to provide customers and individuals with information about the cybersecurity incident on our systems…"

A substitute notice is the legally required public posting used when a company cannot individually notify everyone whose personal data may have been exposed. It is linked from every store's footer, and no one is monitoring what ranks beside it. Dealership breaches reliably attract class-action firms whose pages then rank on the dealership's own name — a reputation exposure an order of magnitude larger than any review score.

2. An unanswered complaint thread on Kia of Vero Beach

A public Facebook post from roughly a month ago, 30+ comments: a recall, a full transmission failure, and a customer left in a rental for more than two months. Still active. Nobody from the store has replied.

3. Six duplicate and phantom listings are splitting your reviews

  • Phil Smith Kia has a second Yelp listing filed under "Lghthse Point," rated 1.0 stars, quoting the same review text as the real 3.0-star profile.
  • Kia of Vero Beach Service exists as a third Vero address, stranding a genuine five-star review where no customer will ever see it.
  • Southern Pines Nissan has no Yelp listing at all, while your own site publishes it as a separate dealership.
  • Phil Smith Mitsubishi has two listings.
  • Southern Pines Chevrolet is published under three different names with four different phone numbers — for one building.
  • A Kia complaint sits on the Toyota of Vero Beach profile; the reviewer names the Kia division himself.

4. Your own published data contradicts itself

  • Phil Smith Kia — Google lists (954) 427-7222; your group site lists (954) 545-7200. Two numbers, one store, from the two sources customers actually use.
  • Vero ZIP conflict — Toyota published as 32962, Kia as 32960. Two buildings thirty seconds apart on the same highway.
  • Five domains across the two Vero rooftops. One of them, toyotaofvero.com, carries live staff email and expires October 20, 2026.
  • Four spellings of the same street on a single page of your website. Citation matching is literal — this is how aggregators create duplicate listings.

5. Some stores are managed. Most are not.

Mark Jacobson Toyota runs an active Facebook page — 6,400 followers, 84% recommend across 476 reviews. Birdeye is deployed and performing at Phil Smith Kia, Southern Pines Kia and Phil Smith Mitsubishi.

Meanwhile both Vero rooftops have unclaimed Birdeye profiles, one listing the store in the wrong state. Southern Pines Kia's own website review page reads "No reviews yet" while the store holds 633 Birdeye reviews at 4.5 stars. The tools are already paid for. Nobody is holding a standard across the group.

6. No store in the group is BBB accredited

Toyota of Vero Beach carries an F, driven by a 41-point licensing alert — and the dealership holds a valid Florida MV4029 licence through September 2027. It is a records gap, not a licensing problem, and it is correctable.

3The Program

The work splits cleanly: a one-time cleanup that removes the structural damage, then an ongoing standard that keeps it from returning.

Phase one — recovery sprint

Six weeks, one time. Every duplicate and phantom listing filed for removal or merge. One canonical name, address and phone per rooftop, propagated to the aggregators that feed everything downstream. The Yelp response backlog cleared at all thirteen stores. Both Vero Birdeye profiles claimed and corrected. The BBB licensing record challenged with your MV4029. A baseline captured per store, so every later gain is provable rather than asserted.

Phase two — ongoing brand management

Every new review answered within 24 hours, on every platform, at every store. Review generation running in the service lane so the volume that beats Yelp's filter actually accumulates. Listings monitored so duplicates never quietly reappear. Monthly reporting per rooftop. Brand-search monitoring — including the breach notice — so nothing ranks beside your name without you knowing first.
