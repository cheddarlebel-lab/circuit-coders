## Part 1 — What We Found: The Technical Audit

### 1. Your Toyota homepage heading says "Visit DealerOn Toyota"
The main heading (H1) on verobeachtoyota.com — the most important on-page signal Google reads — currently says **"Visit DealerOn Toyota in Vero Beach, FL."** DealerOn is your website vendor. Their template placeholder was never replaced with your dealership's name. Google and every visitor have been shown the wrong business name, and no one managing your marketing noticed.

### 2. Identity fragmentation across the web
- **Multiple phone numbers in circulation per store** — we counted five for Toyota and at least four for Kia across your own sites, Toyota.com's official locator, Yelp, Cars.com, and DealerRater. Google cross-references name/address/phone everywhere they appear; inconsistency at this scale suppresses the map-pack rankings that drive service appointments.
- Kia staff emails are published on **toyotaofvero.com — a dead domain that won't load.**
- **verobeachkia.com** — the most natural way a customer types the Kia store's name — serves a "Website Expired" error page.
- **DealerRater lists Kia of Vero Beach at the Toyota store's address.**
- An unclaimed Birdeye profile lists the dealership in **"Florida Ridge, MO" — the wrong city and state.**

### 3. No defense of your home market
You are the **only Toyota dealer in Vero Beach** — a genuine geographic moat. Meanwhile, Bev Smith Toyota (Fort Pierce) operates four dedicated, indexed "Toyota dealer near me" landing pages engineered to capture buyers from your side of the county line. Your sites have zero pages defending Vero Beach, Sebastian, or Indian River County searches.

### 4. Zero content presence
The Toyota blog is a dead link (it redirects to the About page). Between two rooftops, a service operation customers have favorited 23,000+ times on CARFAX, and 8,700+ reviews, you sit on more answerable customer-question material than nearly any business in the county — and none of it exists on your sites. Those searches currently resolve to Bev Smith, Melbourne dealers, and forums.

### What's working (and stays)
- Both DealerOn websites are Toyota/Kia-certified and structurally sound. **We are not proposing a website replacement.**
- Google review volume and ratings are excellent raw material — the problem is everything around them.

**The complaint pattern that matters:** across BBB, DealerRater, CARFAX, and Cars.com, the recurring themes are finance-office disputes (financing terms changed after agreement, add-on products, slow warranty/GAP refunds) and service-pricing surprises (phone quote vs. counter price). Individual salespeople and advisors are consistently praised by name — the people are liked; the *processes* generate the complaints, and nobody is responding to any of it.

**Note on the BBB licensing alert:** the profile cites a "competency license" issue. You hold an active Florida dealer license (MV4029, valid through 9/2027) — this appears to be a BBB records gap, but it's been sitting on your profile unchallenged. It is fixable, and it should be fixed this month.

Every item in both tables above is addressable: claim, correct, consolidate, respond, and put a system behind review generation so the strong platforms keep compounding.
