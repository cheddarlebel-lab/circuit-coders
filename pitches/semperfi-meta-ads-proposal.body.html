1Where You Stand Today

Verified August 4–5, 2026 against your live site and your Google Business Profile.

What is working, and what we are not touching

Your website is genuinely good and this proposal does not propose replacing it. Inventory is live, prices are anchored to real TCGPlayer and eBay market data, graded slabs verify by cert number, and the whole thing loads fast on a phone. That is the landing page. It is already better than what the ads will be sending traffic to at most shops.

The actual gap

  • Nobody is being reached on purpose. Everything you get today is walk-past traffic, word of mouth, and whoever happens to find the site. None of that scales, and none of it can be turned up in a slow week.
  • The two things that sell you are invisible outside the store. "Veteran owned" and "Pokémon and sports cards under one roof" are the two facts that move a local buyer, and neither is being put in front of anyone.
  • Your sell-side is completely unmarketed. People sitting on a collection they want to cash out are a different customer than a buyer, they are worth far more per visit, and right now there is no message aimed at them at all.

Two fixable items on your Google listing

Not part of the ad scope, but they cost nothing to fix and both were verified:

  • Your Google Business Profile lists Saturday opening as "10:10 AM" — an obvious typo that should read 10:00.
  • Meta lets you add a free Veteran-Owned Business badge to your Facebook Page. It takes minutes, it is free, and it makes the claim in the ad self-evidently backed.

2What the Ads Have to Do

A $20-average-ticket local card shop does not need brand awareness. It needs two specific behaviours, and they are bought differently:

JobWho it targetsWhat it is worth
Get buyers in the door Pokémon and sports-card collectors inside a 15-mile drive, plus parents buying for kids — a large and consistently underestimated share of Pokémon spend. Steady, repeatable, low ticket, high frequency.
Get collections brought in People who inherited, outgrew or want to liquidate a collection. Different message, different creative, different intent entirely. Rare, high value per visit, and it restocks your cases.

Both run from day one as separate ads in the same ad set, so within about two weeks you know which one is cheaper to reach in this specific town — and that answer decides where the budget goes for the rest of the year.

3The Creative — Already Built

Built off your own shield mark and the exact brand colours from your site's stylesheet, so the ad and the page it lands on are unmistakably the same business. Meta serves different aspect ratios in different places; supplying all three means nothing gets auto-cropped and your address never gets cut off.

What the creative does on purpose

Veteran owned sits directly under the mark, above the fold in every size — it is the first thing read after the logo. Pokémon and sports cards is the headline, not a bullet, because those two words are what a scrolling collector stops for. Buy / Sell / Trade is given equal weight so the sell-side message is carried even by the buy-side ad. And the address, hours and phone are set at a size that is still legible at thumbnail, because a local ad that does not say where you are is a branding exercise.

4Targeting and Budget

Where

Fallbrook + a 15-mile radius. That ring picks up Bonsall, Rainbow, De Luz, Camp Pendleton, north Vista and the near edge of Temecula — the realistic drive-in catchment for a card shop. We do not run county-wide. San Diego proper is an hour each way and it will eat the budget returning nothing.

Who

  • 18–54 broadly, plus a separate 28–50 parents ad set.
  • Interests: Pokémon Trading Card Game, trading card games, sports memorabilia, Topps, Panini, PSA, collectible card games, baseball cards.
  • Nothing layered on top of that. Over-narrowing inside a 15-mile radius starves delivery and drives your costs up, not down.
  • Retargeting switches on once the pixel has an audience — anyone who browsed inventory and did not come in. Consistently the cheapest visit you will buy.

5The Copy

Three primary-text variants are written and ready. Only the first ~125 characters show before Meta truncates to "See more", so each one is built to land its hook inside that window.

Variant B — lead with sellingSellers

"Sitting on a collection? Bring it in. We make cash offers on Pokémon and sports cards, singles or whole collections, priced off live TCGPlayer and eBay comps — not guesswork. Veteran owned, right here in Fallbrook."

Variant C — shortReels / Stories

"Fallbrook's card shop. Pokémon, sports cards, slabs, sealed. Buy · Sell · Trade. Veteran owned. Open Tue–Sun 10–6."

Headlines rotate across Pokémon & Sports Cards in Fallbrook, Veteran-Owned Card Shop, We Buy Cards — Cash Offers and Buy · Sell · Trade in Fallbrook. Clicks land on your live inventory page, not a generic homepage — people who click a card ad want to see cards.

7Two Things to Confirm First

Confirm before launch

The veteran-owned claim needs to be consistent

The creative says Veteran Owned, as requested. Flagging it once so it is not a surprise: your website's business-facts file still carries veteranOwned: false with a note from June saying it was never confirmed, which is why the site currently renders "Community First" in that slot instead. If the shop has confirmed it, we flip that flag and the site and the ad say the same thing. If for any reason it should not be claimed, tell us and we will rebuild the creative without it — at no charge, before anything runs. An advertising claim that the business's own website contradicts is the one thing worth catching before spend starts.

Hours

The creative says Open Tue–Sun, 10 AM – 6 PM (Monday closed), taken from your Google Business Profile. Worth a quick nod that this is still current before it goes in front of a few thousand people — and the Saturday "10:10 AM" typo on Google is worth correcting at the same time.
