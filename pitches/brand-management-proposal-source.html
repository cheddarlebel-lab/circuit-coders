1What We Found: The Technical Audit

1. Your Toyota homepage heading says "Visit DealerOn Toyota"

The main heading (H1) on verobeachtoyota.com — the most important on-page signal Google reads — currently says "Visit DealerOn Toyota in Vero Beach, FL." DealerOn is your website vendor. Their template placeholder was never replaced with your dealership's name. Google and every visitor have been shown the wrong business name, and nobody noticed.

2. Identity fragmentation across the web

  • Multiple phone numbers in circulation per store — five for Toyota and at least four for Kia across your own sites, Toyota.com's official locator, Yelp, Cars.com, and DealerRater. Google cross-references name/address/phone everywhere they appear; inconsistency at this scale suppresses the map-pack rankings that drive service appointments.
  • Kia staff emails are published on toyotaofvero.com — a dead domain that won't load.
  • verobeachkia.com — the most natural way a customer types the Kia store's name — serves a "Website Expired" error page.
  • DealerRater lists Kia of Vero Beach at the Toyota store's address.
  • An unclaimed Birdeye profile lists the dealership in "Florida Ridge, MO" — the wrong city and state.

3. No defense of your home market

You are the only Toyota dealer in Vero Beach — a genuine geographic moat. Meanwhile, Bev Smith Toyota (Fort Pierce) operates four dedicated, indexed "Toyota dealer near me" landing pages engineered to capture buyers from your side of the county line. Your sites have zero pages defending Vero Beach, Sebastian, or Indian River County searches.

4. Zero content presence

The Toyota blog is a dead link (it redirects to the About page). Between two rooftops, a service operation customers have favorited 23,000+ times on CARFAX, and 8,700+ reviews, you sit on more answerable customer-question material than nearly any business in the county — and none of it exists on your sites. Those searches currently resolve to Bev Smith, Melbourne dealers, and forums.

What's working (and stays): both DealerOn websites are Toyota/Kia-certified and structurally sound — we are not proposing a website replacement — and your advertising program continues untouched. Google review volume and ratings are excellent raw material. The problem is everything around them.

4The Get-Well Plan

Phase 0 — The Get-Well Cleanup (Weeks 1–2) · included in month one

  1. Correct the Toyota homepage H1; audit every heading/meta on both sites (via your DealerOn dashboard — no site migration; DealerOn's turnaround applies to changes that require their support).
  2. BBB triage, both identities: draft and file responses to all open complaints (with your team's input on the facts of each case), open the rating-remediation path, resolve the licensing-record gap, and begin separating the Kia store's BBB identity from the F-rated entity profile. To be straight with you: BBB rating recovery is a months-long process — we manage it and report on it monthly, but the BBB controls its own clock.
  3. Domain cleanup: verify ownership of toyotaofvero.com and verobeachkia.com; redirect or retire what you control, and pursue recovery of anything registered outside the dealership. Correct every staff-email reference either way.
  4. One canonical phone scheme per store, corrected everywhere we can claim or edit — including the Kia wrong-address DealerRater listing. (Toyota.com locator data is updated through your Toyota dealer portal; we prepare the correction, your team submits it.)
  5. Claim what's unclaimed: both Birdeye profiles (fixing the "Missouri" address), file the merge of the duplicate Kia Facebook pages, file removal of the legacy "Toyota Kia" Yelp listing.
  6. Baseline report: rankings, Google Business Profile insights, review velocity for both rooftops — every later claim of progress is measured against day zero.

Deliverable, stated honestly: everything within our direct control is completed within 14 days of access being in place. Every item that depends on a third party's process — BBB, Facebook, Yelp, DealerOn, domain registrars — is filed within those 14 days and tracked weekly until closed, with status in every monthly report.

Phase 1 — Foundations (Days 15–45)

  • Full citation cleanup across both rooftops (50+ directories and data aggregators).
  • Google Business Profile takeover, both stores: services, categories, photos, Q&A seeding, weekly posts.
  • Review response system, every platform: all new reviews answered within 24 hours — including negative ones, which is where shoppers actually look. Backlog of unanswered negatives addressed with a consistent, non-defensive voice.
  • Review generation engine: post-sale and post-service prompts with store routing — pointed at the platforms that need volume: Kia's empty CARFAX and weak Yelp. We build and run the system; the one thing we need from your floor is a 10-second step at delivery and service pickup (a card or text with the right link), and we'll train your team on it.

Phase 2 — Offense (Days 45–90)

  • Defensive local pages: Vero Beach, Sebastian, Indian River County × sales/service/parts intent — the wall against Bev Smith's near-me strategy.
  • Content engine launch: 4 pieces/month answering the questions your customers already ask, each targeted at searches currently resolving to competitors.

Ongoing (Month 4+)

Monthly cadence per rooftop: Google Business Profile posts and full review management · 2+ local/content pages · rank, GBP, and review-velocity reporting · quarterly competitive audit (we watch Bev Smith and the Melbourne dealers so you don't have to).
